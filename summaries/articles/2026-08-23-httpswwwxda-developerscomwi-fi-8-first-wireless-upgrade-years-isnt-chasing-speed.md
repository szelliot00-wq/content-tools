# Wi-Fi 8 is the first wireless upgrade in years that isn't chasing speed

Source: https://www.xda-developers.com/wi-fi-8-first-wireless-upgrade-years-isnt-chasing-speed-home-networks-need-it/

## Summary
Wi-Fi 8, dubbed "Ultra High Reliability" by the IEEE, marks a significant departure from previous Wi-Fi generations by prioritizing reliability and real-world performance over raw speed increases. Unlike past upgrades that chased ever-higher theoretical throughput, Wi-Fi 8 targets a 25% improvement in throughput under interference, 25% lower latency, and a 25% reduction in packet loss. The standard is expected to be finalized in May 2028, positioning it as a direct competitor to emerging 6G cellular networks.

## Key takeaways
- Wi-Fi 8 keeps roughly the same maximum speeds as Wi-Fi 7 (same bands, modulation, channel bandwidth, and spatial streams) — the focus is quality, not quantity.
- The IEEE's stated goals are a 25% improvement in throughput at various signal-to-noise levels, 25% lower latency (95th percentile), and 25% fewer packet data unit losses.
- New technologies include Distributed-tone Resource Units (DRUs) for better range on low-power devices, interference mitigation pilots, unequal modulations per spatial stream, and P-EDCA for priority channel access.
- Seamless roaming between access points and improved multi-AP coordination are explicitly addressed, benefiting mesh network users.
- The reliability focus is driven by the explosion of smart home devices, which strain networks even without needing high bandwidth.
- The standard finalizes in May 2028; the article suggests waiting for Wi-Fi 8 devices rather than investing in Wi-Fi 7 now, especially if you lack multi-gigabit internet service.
