# More than half of adults in U.S. say they lack basic statistical understanding

Source: https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/more-half-adults-us-say-they-lack-basic-statistical-understanding

## Summary
A Penn State study published in PLOS One found that 62% of U.S. adults report little or no understanding of basic statistical concepts, including p-values, while only 11% use statistics regularly in daily life. Despite this gap, 9 out of 10 respondents said they would rely on statistics more in their decision-making if they understood the subject better. Researcher Mark Ramos argues that statistical literacy is achievable and critical for navigating news, social media, and policy debates.

## Key takeaways
- 62% of the 1,000 survey respondents reported no or limited statistical knowledge; only 11% use statistics regularly.
- 25% had no understanding of statistics at all; 37% described themselves as having only limited familiarity.
- 90% of respondents said they would use statistics more in decision-making if they understood it better, indicating strong latent demand for statistical literacy.
- Statistics, including p-values, are not inherently difficult — they are grounded in intuition — but are easy to misunderstand without a proper introduction.
- Statistical literacy is framed as a civic skill: understanding evidence quality helps people critically evaluate research cited in news reports, social media, and policy debates.
- Free educational resources exist, including Penn State's publicly available STAT 200: Elementary Statistics course materials, for those who want to improve their understanding.
