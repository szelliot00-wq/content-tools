# Our Solar System Is Terminally Unstable

Source: https://www.404media.co/our-solar-system-is-terminally-unstable-and-will-be-completely-destroyed-study-finds/

## Summary
This 404 Media "Abstract" column by Becky Ferreira covers four recent scientific studies published around October 3, 2026. The headline finding is a new study dramatically shortening the estimated lifespan of our outer solar system from 100 billion years to roughly 6 billion years, driven by chaos triggered during the Sun's death. The column also touches on new evidence for animal life predating the Ediacaran period, Earth's shifting poles, and formaldehyde rain on ancient Mars.

## Key takeaways
- **Solar system lifespan slashed**: A study led by Konstantin Batygin (Caltech) found the outer planets are likely destabilized during the Sun's red giant phase and won't survive more than ~1 billion years after the Sun becomes a white dwarf — far shorter than the previously assumed 100 billion years.
- **The Sun itself is the culprit**: Stochastic mass loss as the Sun dies shakes the planetary system apart, rather than gradual chaos or a passing star, as Newton and others had suspected.
- **Ancient animal origins**: Molecular clock analysis suggests the first animals may have appeared ~800 million years ago (Tonian era), predating the Ediacaran fossil record by over 200 million years — meaning early sponges and comb jellies likely survived "Snowball Earth."
- **Earth's poles wander fast**: New research confirms episodic rapid true polar wander (TPW) over the past 320 million years, shifting poles thousands of miles, with human activity (dams, glacial melt) now also contributing slightly.
- **Formaldehyde rained on Mars**: Models of ancient Mars (3.6–3.8 billion years ago) show formaldehyde — a precursor to life-building molecules — likely rained into Martian waterways, pointing to potential landing sites for future life-hunting missions.
