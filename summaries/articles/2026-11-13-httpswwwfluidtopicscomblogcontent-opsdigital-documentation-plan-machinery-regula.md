# Your Digital Documentation Plan for Machinery Regulation Compliance

Source: https://www.fluidtopics.com/blog/content-ops/digital-documentation-plan-machinery-regulation/

## Summary
Published November 12, 2026, this article from Fluid Topics outlines how machinery manufacturers and technical documentation teams can prepare for EU Regulation 2023/1230, which takes effect January 20, 2027. It presents an eight-step compliance plan covering everything from auditing existing documentation to establishing long-term retention processes. The article also highlights how digital delivery platforms can help teams meet the regulation's requirements for findability, language accessibility, and 10-year availability.

## Key takeaways
- **EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 applies from January 20, 2027** and requires digital instructions to cover reasonably foreseeable misuse, be accessible online, downloadable, printable, and available for at least 10 years after market launch.
- **Digital delivery is the permitted default**, but simply posting a PDF is not enough — instructions must be findable, versioned, and user-accessible in the correct language for each market.
- **Paper instructions are still required in two cases**: when a user requests them at purchase, and for safety information on machinery that non-professionals may operate.
- **The eight-step compliance plan**: (1) audit existing docs, (2) map content to regulatory requirements, (3) establish cross-team governance, (4) plan digital access delivery, (5) manage product variants/configurations, (6) align translations, (7) build an update process, and (8) retain historical documentation.
- **Cross-team governance is critical** — engineering, compliance, product, and service teams all contribute to the required technical file, and unclear ownership leads to version drift and failed compliance.
- **QR codes on machines** should link users directly to product-specific instructions filtered by model and version, reducing the need to search through irrelevant content.
- **Translation management** must be treated as safety-critical — instructions must be available in the language(s) required by each EU Member State where the machinery is sold or put into service.
- **Documentation updates must be integrated into product change processes**, not treated as a separate project, to keep instructions accurate as machines evolve over their lifecycle.
