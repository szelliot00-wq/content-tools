# Goodbye Google

Source: https://robert.ocallahan.org/2026/09/goodbye-google.html

## Summary
Robert O'Callahan, a long-time tech industry veteran living in New Zealand, has resigned from Google. His decision stems from a principled objection to his team's work accelerating AI development, which he believes is progressing too rapidly and poses genuine risks to humanity. Despite valuing his colleagues and compensation, he found it impossible to transfer to a non-AI role at Google given his New Zealand location, so he chose to leave entirely to return to his own projects.

## Key takeaways
- O'Callahan quit Google because his team's goal was making AI cheaper and lower-latency, which he believes is harmful given the current pace of AI development.
- He views AI risk as real and uncertain — not certain doom, but alarming enough that pursuing ASI in the near term is "inherently irresponsible."
- He notes that many "doomer" predictions have already materialized (reward hacking, misalignment, deceptive models, eval awareness, psychotic swarms), lending credibility to broader concerns.
- Beyond existential risk, he is also concerned about "cognitive surrender" — people outsourcing thinking to AI in ways that erode human capability.
- Geographic constraints (Google limits engineering work in New Zealand) made an internal transfer off AI projects effectively impossible, leaving resignation as his only option.
- He plans to return to his own projects, Pernosco and rr (developer tooling), and is active as a lay preacher at Auckland Chinese Presbyterian Church.
