# Cognitive Mercantilism: AI panic resembles fears of early capitalism

Source: https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/cognitive-mercantilism

## Summary
Venkatesh Rao draws a parallel between historical mercantilism — the belief that wealth is finite and must be hoarded — and contemporary anxieties about AI. Just as mercantilists failed to anticipate how capitalism would create new wealth and demand rather than merely redistribute scarce resources, today's AI pessimists assume intelligence is a fixed, zero-sum commodity. Rao argues that abundant AI may instead trigger a "cognitive industrious revolution," expanding both the supply of intelligence and, crucially, human demand for it, much as cheap consumer goods spurred ordinary people to work harder and want more in early modern England.

## Key takeaways
- **Cognitive mercantilism mirrors historical mercantilism**: Fears that AI will cause a zero-sum fight over scarce intelligence echo the pre-capitalist belief that wealth was finite and had to be hoarded or conquered.
- **Demand for intelligence is elastic, not fixed**: Just as cheap goods created new consumer appetites in early capitalism, abundant AI may unlock entirely new categories of cognitive demand that don't exist yet.
- **The "AI Malthusian" error**: Worrying that AI intelligence will outpace humanity's ability to absorb it repeats the Malthusian mistake of underestimating human adaptability and the expansion of desire.
- **A "cognitive industrious revolution" may precede an AI industrial revolution**: People may voluntarily invest more cognitive effort when AI lowers the barrier to pursuing ambitious projects — as the author notes about his own increased productivity.
- **Cognitive mercantilism serves elites, not innovators**: Historically, mercantilism was a philosophy of ruling elites, not merchants. Today's AI gatekeeping similarly reflects rentier attitudes rather than entrepreneurial ones.
- **AI systems as potential demand sources**: Just as subsistence farmers became consumers under capitalism, AI agents could eventually become autonomous participants in a cognitive economy, not merely producers serving humans.
- **The biggest win may be liberation, not efficiency**: As the agricultural revolution freed most people from farming, the AI revolution's deepest impact may be freeing minds from subsistence-level cognitive labor to pursue richer intellectual lives.
