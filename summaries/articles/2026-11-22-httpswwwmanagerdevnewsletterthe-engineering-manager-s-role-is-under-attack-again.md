# The Engineering Manager's role is under attack (again)

Source: https://www.manager.dev/newsletter/the-engineering-manager-s-role-is-under-attack-again

## Summary
The article, co-authored by Anton Zaides and organizational development consultant Suzan Bond, argues that the current wave of engineering manager layoffs and "flattening" is a cyclical mistake companies keep repeating. Drawing on historical examples from Google and Microsoft, it shows that removing managers consistently leads to organizational dysfunction — and that companies quietly rebuild those layers after experiencing the damage. The piece frames AI-driven lean-staffing pressure as the latest catalyst for this recurring delusion, and offers EMs practical advice for making their value visible to senior leadership.

## Key takeaways
- The flattening cycle is not new: Google tried removing managers in 2002, ran Project Oxygen in 2008 to confirm managers matter, and is now flattening again — companies never seem to learn.
- Removing managers creates "relational debt" — eroded trust, psychological safety, and collaboration — which compounds over months or years before leadership notices the damage.
- AI is making things worse by reducing human contact within teams, increasing the risk of toxic behavior and conflict that managers would normally mediate.
- Senior leaders are being pressured by market narratives ("go leaner with AI") and are copying peers without evaluating whether their own conditions are the same.
- EMs can fight back by writing down the 10–15 small people problems they see and translating them into business-impact language that senior leaders actually care about.
- Span-of-control extremes (Meta targeting 1:50 ratios, 15–20 reports becoming common) combined with coding expectations make the EM role unsustainable.
- The recovery from flattening is always quiet and gradual — no headlines, just slowly rebuilding the layers that were cut.
- The authors connect Meta's 2024 layoff of 700 EMs to its subsequent resignation wave as a concrete, recent example of the harm.
