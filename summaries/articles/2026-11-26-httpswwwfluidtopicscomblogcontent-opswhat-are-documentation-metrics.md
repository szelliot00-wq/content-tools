# What Are Documentation Metrics and Why Do They Matter?

Source: https://www.fluidtopics.com/blog/content-ops/what-are-documentation-metrics/

## Summary
Documentation metrics are quantitative and qualitative measures that track how content is used and whether users find it valuable. Despite their importance — 88% of decision makers review docs during the sales process — a quarter of businesses track no documentation metrics at all, and those that do monitor only 2.7 indicators on average. The article argues that a robust metrics practice covering accuracy, relevance, clarity, helpfulness, and user trust enables teams to prioritize work, reduce costs, and demonstrate ROI to leadership.

## Key takeaways
- Most teams are flying blind: 25% track no documentation metrics, and 49% don't track internal metrics at all.
- Effective documentation metrics cover five dimensions: accuracy, relevance, clarity, helpfulness, and user trust.
- Dedicated content analytics (search behavior, user journeys, ratings, AI interactions) reveal details that general web analytics miss.
- Four groups benefit from documentation data: documentation/knowledge teams, support teams, product teams, and executives — each using it differently.
- Seven business benefits include: improved employee productivity, better customer self-service, reduced costs, faster knowledge gap identification, better SEO/GEO, optimized AI experiences, and proven ROI.
- Zero-result searches are a direct signal of knowledge gaps — a practical starting point for teams new to metrics.
- Getting started means picking metrics that cover the five dimensions, sharing results cross-functionally, and running a repeating cycle: collect, analyze, prioritize, change, remeasure.
