# What is the size of Yemen? (2024)

Source: https://theborys.substack.com/p/what-is-the-size-of-yemen

## Summary
A researcher discovered that Yemen's widely cited area of 555,000 km2 (and even the CIA/UN figure of 527,968 km2) are both wrong — Google Maps measurement yields roughly 456,000 km2. Tracing the error back through historical sources, they found that both North and South Yemen's sizes were independently inflated due to separate century-old mistakes, and when the two countries unified in 1990, the flawed numbers were simply added together. The errors have propagated unchecked through Wikipedia, the CIA World Factbook, the UN, and the World Bank for over 100 years.

## Key takeaways
- The actual area of Yemen is approximately 456,000 km2, not the commonly cited 527,968 km2 or 555,000 km2.
- North Yemen's inflated figure of 195,000 km2 originated from a 1904 source describing the much larger Ottoman Yemeni Vilayet, not independent North Yemen — and was copied without fact-checking for nearly a century.
- South Yemen's figure was inflated because a 1976 World Bank report double-counted one administrative region (Thamud), adding tens of thousands of km2 that didn't exist.
- The 555,000 km2 figure likely came from a 1991 Yemeni statistical yearbook that erroneously extended the northern border to the 20th parallel, possibly for propaganda purposes before the Saudi border was formally demarcated in 2000.
- Authoritative institutions (CIA, UN, World Bank) all perpetuated these errors by copying earlier sources rather than independently verifying the figures.
- Even today, virtually every online statistic about Yemen's size is incorrect.
