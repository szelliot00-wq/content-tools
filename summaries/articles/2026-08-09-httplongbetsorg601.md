# The original URL for this prediction will no longer be available in 11 years (2011)

Source: http://longbets.org/601/

## Summary
This is a Long Bets prediction (#601) made in 2011 by Jeremy Keith, who bet $1,000 that the URL `www.longbets.org/601` would no longer be accessible in eleven years (by 2022). His challenger, Matt Haughey, took the opposing position — that the URL would still work. The page indicates Haughey won, meaning the URL did survive until February 22, 2022.

## Key takeaways
- **The bet:** Jeremy Keith predicted link rot would kill the URL within 11 years; Matt Haughey bet it would survive.
- **The outcome:** Haughey won — the URL remained accessible on February 22, 2022, the designated test date.
- **Keith's reasoning** drew on Tim Berners-Lee's "Cool URIs don't change" principle, arguing that link rot makes long-term URL survival unlikely even over relatively short periods.
- **Haughey's reasoning** was that the web has matured past its boom-and-bust phase, and stable URL schemes with 301 redirects are now achievable for anyone with moderate technical knowledge.
- **Stakes:** $1,000 — going to Bletchley Park Trust if Keith won, or The Internet Archive if Haughey won (so The Internet Archive received the funds).
- The result is a small but concrete data point against the assumption that link rot is inevitable over an 11-year horizon for maintained sites.
