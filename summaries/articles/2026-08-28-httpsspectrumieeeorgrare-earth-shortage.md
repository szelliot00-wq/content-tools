# A Looming Rare Earth Shortage Threatens Critical Tech

Source: https://spectrum.ieee.org/rare-earth-shortage

## Summary
China holds a near-total monopoly on the production of erbium and yttrium — two rare earth elements critical to fiber-optic communications, jet engine turbine coatings, data center power infrastructure, and consumer electronics. Chinese export restrictions imposed in 2025 are already squeezing U.S. supply, with yttrium shipments down roughly 75% in 2026, and a key deadline in November 2026 could trigger a full cutoff. Despite the severity of the risk, Western governments and industry have been slow to develop alternative supply chains.

## Key takeaways
- **Erbium** is irreplaceable in fiber-optic amplifiers (used every 50–120 km along undersea and terrestrial cables) because it re-radiates at exactly the 1,550 nm wavelength that silica glass transmits most efficiently — no substitute exists at commercial scale outside China.
- **Yttrium** is essential for turbine blade coatings (yttria-stabilized zirconia) in jet engines and gas turbines, the latter of which are in record demand to power AI data centers; it also appears in LEDs, microwave filters, solid-state lasers, and ceramic capacitors.
- China controls ~90% of global yttrium mining and essentially 100% of processing for both elements; no commercial-scale erbium oxide production exists outside China.
- Yttrium shipments to the U.S. are already down ~75% in 2026 vs. 2025; manufacturers like Oerlikon Metco report supply chaos, and GE Vernova's yttrium stockpile timeline is unclear.
- A November 2026 deadline determines whether China extends or removes the export suspensions — the outcome is tied to broader U.S.–China trade negotiations involving GPUs, chip equipment, and other dual-use technologies.
- The U.S. government launched **Project Vault**, a $12 billion stockpiling initiative (including a record $10B Export-Import Bank loan), but critics say the West is still "lagging behind the geopolitics" with no serious domestic production plans for either element.
