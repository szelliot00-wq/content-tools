# Who gets to define the rules for AI?

Source: https://cohere.com/blog/who-gets-to-define-the-rules-for-ai

## Summary
Written by Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez on September 13, 2026, this article argues against allowing a small group of dominant Silicon Valley AI companies to write the safety rules and standards for the entire AI industry. Gomez contends that Anthropic's recent proposal — which seeks antitrust exemptions so frontier labs can coordinate on safety standards — is functionally a cartel that would entrench incumbents while excluding smaller companies, other nations, and the public. He draws parallels to past regulatory failures (credit rating agencies, European auto regulations) where safety justifications were used to lock in market dominance. As an alternative, he proposes an open, evidence-based, internationally inclusive regulatory framework.

## Key takeaways
- **The cartel concern:** Letting a few dominant US AI labs jointly set safety standards — with government-granted antitrust waivers — mirrors historical cases where "safety" framing created anti-competitive monopolies.
- **Who's excluded matters:** Anthropic's proposal would bind all developers to standards written by a handful of companies, with no public comment, no vote, and no input from smaller labs, open-source developers, academics, or other governments.
- **Fear as a tool:** Existential risk narratives (e.g., "10% chance of human extinction") are gut feelings dressed as data, and focusing on hypothetical superintelligence distracts from real, present harms like voice-cloning fraud.
- **Rules should follow capability, not company size:** A dangerous AI capability should be regulated the same whether it comes from a trillion-dollar lab or a university — current proposals create compliance burdens only large incumbents can absorb.
- **Four pillars for better regulation:** Evidence-based risk frameworks, mandatory transparency, scoped independent testing, and truly independent assurance mechanisms modeled on financial, aviation, and nuclear oversight.
- **Sovereignty and competition matter:** Governments and critical infrastructure operators need AI they can run on their own infrastructure, from providers they can replace — not a foreign monopoly behind a closed interface.
- **Inclusion is the legitimacy test:** A trustworthy rulebook requires voices that would rule against even Cohere — academics, civil society, smaller labs, and governments with no stake in the outcome.
