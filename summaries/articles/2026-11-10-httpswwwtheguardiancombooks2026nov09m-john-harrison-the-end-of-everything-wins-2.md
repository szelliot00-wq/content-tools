# M John Harrison becomes first science fiction author to win the Booker prize

Source: https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/nov/09/m-john-harrison-the-end-of-everything-wins-2026-booker-prize

## Summary
M John Harrison, age 81, won the 2026 Booker Prize for his postapocalyptic novel *The End of Everything*, becoming both the oldest winner in the prize's history and the first author from the science fiction world to take the award. Set on a near-future Kent coast, the novel follows two characters navigating a world transformed by an incomprehensible disaster and the presence of mysterious shape-changing beings. The judges reached a unanimous decision, with chair Mary Beard praising the book as one that "turns you upside down" while reflecting "the politics of now seen through the lens of the future."

## Key takeaways
- Harrison is the first Booker winner to have emerged from the "bona fide SF world," marking a historic moment for genre fiction's mainstream recognition.
- At 81, he is the oldest winner in Booker Prize history, surpassing Margaret Atwood (79 at her 2019 win).
- The novel is a deliberately challenging read — only 198 pages — in which the disaster at its center is never fully explained, mirroring what Harrison calls "the collapse of rationality that comes with Trumpism and the internet."
- The mysterious alien beings (the "iGhetti") are interpreted as paralleling gentrification and, in the age of AI, hinting at something digital.
- Harrison has been publishing for six decades and previously won the Arthur C Clarke Award and Goldsmiths Prize, but this is his first Booker nomination.
- The win is also a first for his publisher, Serpent's Tail (an imprint of Profile Books), after five nominations.
