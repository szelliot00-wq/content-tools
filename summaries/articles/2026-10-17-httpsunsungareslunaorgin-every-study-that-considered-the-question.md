# "In every study that considered the question"

Source: https://unsung.aresluna.org/in-every-study-that-considered-the-question/

## Summary
The article draws a parallel between confusing car light iconography (which may cause drivers to use parking lights instead of headlights in bad weather) and Apple's increasing shift from text labels to icons in iOS and keyboard UIs. The author argues, citing Jef Raskin's research, that icons are consistently harder to understand than text labels — especially on first encounter. Apple's move toward iconography appears driven partly by space constraints introduced by the "liquid glass" design aesthetic, but it introduces new ambiguity since many icons (checkmarks, ×, loupes) carry multiple meanings.

## Key takeaways
- Research shows icons are more difficult to understand than text labels, especially for new users — contradicting the common assumption that icons are more intuitive.
- Poor iconography has real-world consequences: drivers likely turned on the wrong car lights in rain because the light selector icons are ambiguous and feedback is indistinguishable.
- Apple is replacing text labels (Cancel, Done, Return) with icons across iOS 26/27 and new keyboards, likely due to space pressure from the liquid glass design rather than purely aesthetic choice.
- Overloaded icons (checkmark, ×, loupe) create new ambiguity because each symbol carries multiple possible meanings depending on context.
- Icons work best when they are universally recognized, used consistently to build familiarity, or paired with text — Apple is inconsistently applying its new edit icon, missing opportunities to teach users its meaning.
- Digital dashboards in modern cars open the door to tooltip-style feedback (e.g., showing "parking lights" in text when a stalk is rotated), which could help bridge the gap between icon and meaning.
