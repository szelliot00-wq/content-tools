# Slovakia finds Russian backdoor in traffic speed cameras

Source: https://risky.biz/risky-bulletin-slovakia-finds-russian-backdoor-in-traffic-speed-cameras/

## Summary
Slovakia's national security service (NBU) discovered a Russian-origin backdoor in NERO R-ONE traffic speed cameras purchased as part of a €30 million EU-funded infrastructure project. The cameras, rebranded versions of a Russian CORDON PRO.M model made by St. Petersburg firm Semicon, contain an SMS-triggered backdoor that grants shell and network access when contacted from hardcoded Russian phone numbers. The NBU also found multiple additional security flaws including disabled SecureBoot, vulnerable web management portals, and unauthenticated live stream access. The Interior Ministry paused deployment of 279 cameras pending an independent audit.

## Key takeaways
- The cameras were acquired through a Cyprus shell company with fake certifications, and the Interior Ministry initially denied their Russian origin.
- The backdoor is triggered via SMS from hardcoded Russian phone numbers, granting full shell and network access to the devices.
- SecureBoot was disabled on the devices, meaning firmware integrity is never verified — a foundational supply-chain security failure.
- Similar devices may be deployed in Croatia and other Eastern European countries, suggesting broader regional exposure.
