# This I Believe (1952)

Source: https://www.awakin.org/v2/read/view.php?tid=468

## Summary
Written in 1952, Robert A. Heinlein's "This I Believe" is a personal essay affirming his faith in the fundamental goodness of ordinary people. Drawing on everyday examples — a generous priest, a selfless veterinarian, honest craftsmen, and charitable strangers — Heinlein argues that decency vastly outnumbers cruelty in the world, even if headlines suggest otherwise. He closes with a sweeping declaration of belief in all of humanity's honesty, courage, and durability, expressing confidence that the human race will not only survive but eventually reach the stars.

## Key takeaways
- Ordinary people's quiet virtues — kindness, generosity, honesty — far outweigh the faults that dominate the news cycle.
- Decency is pervasive but unreported; for every headline crime, tens of thousands of honest, caring acts go unnoticed.
- Trustworthy institutions and systems (government, trade, infrastructure) exist because most people — including politicians and craftsmen — do their jobs with integrity.
- Heinlein's belief extends across all races and peoples, grounding his optimism in a universal human character rather than any one group.
- Humanity's defining traits — curiosity, courage, and decency — give Heinlein confidence that the species will endure and expand far beyond Earth.
