# NASA to keep its 48-year-old Voyager 2 probe running for yet another year

Source: https://www.space.com/space-exploration/voyager/nasa-figured-out-how-to-keep-its-48-year-old-voyager-2-probe-running-for-yet-another-year

## Summary
NASA has extended the operational life of its 48-year-old Voyager 2 spacecraft by implementing power-saving changes dubbed the "Big Bang." Engineers reduced power consumption by disabling non-science devices and switching to lower-power alternatives for keeping the craft warm, allowing all three remaining science instruments to continue operating for at least another year. The same changes are planned for Voyager 1 in the coming months.

## Key takeaways
- Voyager 2's nuclear power source (a radioisotope thermoelectric generator) loses about 4 watts per year, forcing ongoing power conservation measures.
- The "Big Bang" power adjustment turned off non-essential, non-science devices and replaced some systems with lower-power alternatives.
- Voyager 2 now operates only 3 of its original 10 science instruments; two were shut down in 2024 alone due to power constraints.
- Without this intervention, one of the three remaining instruments would have had to be shut down later this year.
- Voyager 2 is currently ~142 astronomical units from Earth; Voyager 1 is ~171 AU away — signals take nearly 24 hours one-way.
- A similar "Big Bang" power change is planned for Voyager 1 in the coming months.
- Both probes launched in 1977 to exploit a rare planetary alignment and conducted historic flybys of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
