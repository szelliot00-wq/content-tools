# Reasons robotics is hard

Source: https://secondthoughts.ai/p/14-reasons-robotics-is-hard

## Summary
Steve Newman's article argues that while AI is advancing rapidly in knowledge work, the path to broadly capable humanoid robots faces 14 distinct and serious technical, physical, and societal challenges. Demo videos are an unreliable measure of progress because they can cherry-pick successes, hide failure rates, and avoid difficult scenarios. The article catalogs what robots still need to master before they can function as general-purpose workers outside controlled environments.

## Key takeaways
- **Demo videos mislead**: They may show cherry-picked successes, edited footage, or carefully staged scenarios — not representative real-world performance.
- **Hands and dexterity remain unsolved**: No robot hand matches the human hand's combination of flexibility, sensitivity, strength, and reliability.
- **Visual understanding is incomplete**: Robots still struggle to interpret complex scenes, locate grasp points, and navigate cluttered environments.
- **Planning and replanning are hard**: General tasks require breaking work into steps and adapting when surprises occur (a stuck bolt, a child running in).
- **Context and common sense are lacking**: Robots need situational knowledge and judgment that LLMs partially provide but physical tasks demand more.
- **Speed, strength, and mobility lag**: Most humanoid robots move slowly, carry little weight, and struggle with stairs or uneven terrain.
- **Safety is a near-limitless concern**: A malfunctioning robot can cause harm in countless ways that are harder to guard against than software agents.
- **Endurance and overheating**: Robots run only a few hours per charge and generate roughly twice the heat of a human with fewer ways to dissipate it.
- **Generalization fails at edge cases**: Like Waymo driving into flooded roads, robots trained for controlled environments break down in the real world's long tail of unusual situations.
- **Compute trade-offs**: Onboard compute adds weight and heat; cloud-based compute introduces latency and failure modes.
- **Scaling takes years**: Unlike software (ChatGPT hit 100M users in 2 months), physical robot manufacturing supply chains don't yet exist and will take years to build out.
- **Societal and political friction**: Job displacement fears, liability, privacy concerns, and security risks (robots as criminal tools) will constrain deployment even once the technology matures.
