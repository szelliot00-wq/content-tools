# AI Answer Engines: What Do Documentation Teams Need to Know?

Source: https://www.fluidtopics.com/blog/ai/ai-answer-engines-for-documentation-teams/

## Summary
AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini) respond to queries using natural language processing and LLMs, either drawing on training data or real-time web searches, and increasingly cite external sources in their responses. This creates new challenges for documentation teams who must now optimize not just for traditional search (SEO) but for AI citation — a practice called Generative Engine Optimization (GEO). The article outlines how these engines evaluate and select content, and how documentation teams can improve their chances of being cited.

## Key takeaways
- AI answer engines identify query intent, retrieve relevant information, and return conversational summaries with source citations — roughly 50% of retrieved URLs appear in ChatGPT's final responses.
- 31% of pages cited by AI answer engines don't rank in Google at all, meaning traditional SEO alone is insufficient for AI visibility.
- Third-party mentions (G2, "best of" lists, reviews) increase AI trust in your content more than first-party claims.
- Use a question-and-answer format in documentation to match how users query AI engines and how engines structure their replies.
- Structure content for machine readability — granular, well-organized content is easier for AI to extract and reference.
- Recency matters: recently published (or recently updated with a refreshed publish date) documentation is cited more frequently.
- Each AI engine uses its own algorithm and prefers different source types (e.g., Reddit, Wikipedia, G2 vs. Google top-10 results), so knowing your audience's preferred platform helps prioritize GEO efforts.
- Measure GEO citations and AI-referred traffic to track ROI and guide ongoing improvements.
