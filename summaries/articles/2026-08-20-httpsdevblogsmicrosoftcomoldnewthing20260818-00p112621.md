# Why Microsoft Entertainment Pack had a sticker announcing that it had Tetris?

Source: https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20260818-00/?p=112621

## Summary
The first Microsoft Entertainment Pack for Windows shipped without any mention of Tetris on the box art because the Tetris licensing deal hadn't been finalized when boxes went to print. Rather than risk printing boxes featuring a game they might not be able to include, Microsoft prepared red stickers in advance and applied them once the deal closed. Later print runs incorporated Tetris directly into the box art and even rebranded as "Entertainment Pack 1" after sequels had already shipped.

## Key takeaways
- The Tetris sticker existed because licensing negotiations were still ongoing when the box art was finalized — Microsoft didn't want to commit to featuring it in print.
- This was a deliberate risk-mitigation strategy: produce neutral boxes, have stickers ready, apply them only if the deal succeeded.
- Copies with the red sticker are considered rare collector's items, similar to other unusual early Microsoft packaging (like the Windows 95 hologram baby box).
- The first pack had no number in its name because nobody anticipated sequels; later reprints added "1" after packs 2–4 were already on shelves.
