# Don't be a meat proxy

Source: https://gruhn.me/blog/2026-08-03/

## Summary
"Don't be a meat proxy" argues against the emerging pattern of humans acting as mindless relay agents between AI tools and the rest of the world — copying inputs in, pasting outputs out, without actually reading, understanding, or evaluating what the AI produces. The author uses code review as a concrete example: it's now possible to ship code by copy/pasting a ticket into Claude Code, never reading the output, and iterating purely on reviewer feedback — also via copy/paste. The piece warns that this hollow participation strips humans of the judgment and accountability that made their involvement meaningful in the first place.

## Key takeaways
- A "meat proxy" is a human who adds no cognitive value — just shuttling prompts and responses between an AI and the rest of the world.
- The ease of AI tools creates a temptation to skip understanding entirely: copy ticket in, paste code out, repeat on feedback.
- Code review is a specific casualty — it's possible to ship code you've never read, which defeats the purpose of human oversight.
- The value humans provide isn't the act of submitting a prompt; it's the judgment applied before, during, and after AI involvement.
- Even in highly automated workflows (the NATS infrastructure example suggests the author works on complex systems), humans need to genuinely understand what the AI is doing, not just relay its output.
