# What Liberal Arts Education Is for (2024)

Source: https://innig.net/teaching/liberal-arts-manifesto

## Summary
Paul Cantrell, a software developer and liberal arts CS professor, argues that liberal arts education is not synonymous with humanities — it is any education rooted in curiosity, critical thinking, and preparation for an unknowable future. Drawing on how a Religious Studies course unexpectedly sharpened his close-reading skills as a developer, he contends that the distinction that matters is not *what* subject you study but *how* you study it. He traces the term "liberal arts" to its Latin origin (*artes liberales* — skills worthy of a free person) and makes a pointed political argument: a society that reserves broad, exploratory education for the privileged while pushing purely vocational training on marginalized students is effectively deciding who gets to be fully human.

## Key takeaways
- "Liberal arts" does not mean humanities — it describes an *approach* to learning (curiosity, close reading, questioning, synthesis) that can apply to any subject, including computer science.
- Vocational education (specific, immediately practical skills) and liberal arts education are complementary, not opposed; both matter and neither alone is sufficient.
- The historic origin of "liberal arts" — education for free, self-determining people — exposes a troubling implication: pushing purely vocational STEM on disadvantaged students treats them as future workers, not whole human beings.
- Curiosity is the core driver of liberal arts learning; structures that thwart student curiosity undermine the entire purpose, regardless of the subject matter.
- Disciplines transfer in unexpected ways — the *patterns of thought* from a course matter far more than the specific content, which is often forgotten.
- Forcing marginalized groups into STEM pipelines primarily serves employer demand for cheaper labor; it is not the same as liberation or equity.
- Education happens everywhere, not just in school, and everyone deserves access to the kind of learning that prepares them to shape an unknown future.
