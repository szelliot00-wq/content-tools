# 1,741 "informed" consents with one click? GDPR complaint filed

Source: https://noyb.eu/en/1741-informed-consents-one-click-gdpr-complaint-against-dictcc-filed

## Summary
Privacy advocacy group noyb filed a GDPR complaint against the popular online dictionary dict.cc, which presents users with a single-click consent banner covering 1,741 advertising "partner" companies. Fully reading all those companies' privacy policies would take approximately 170 hours, making truly informed consent impossible. The complaint, filed with the Austrian Data Protection Authority, argues dict.cc lacks a valid legal basis for data processing and requests deletion of unlawfully collected data, notification to all data recipients, and a fine to deter future violations.

## Key takeaways
- dict.cc's cookie consent banner bundles 1,741 third-party "partners" into a single click, violating GDPR's requirement that consent be informed, specific, and unambiguous.
- Reading all 1,741 companies' privacy policies would take over 170 hours — more than a full work week — for a single consent decision.
- GDPR mandates freely given, informed, specific, and unambiguous consent; mass bundled consent banners like this are fundamentally incompatible with those requirements.
- This is not isolated to dict.cc — sites like repubblica.it, bergfex.de, and fifa.com use similar practices, highlighting a systemic industry problem 8 years after GDPR took effect.
- Tracking data collected for advertising is frequently sold to data brokers and purchased by law enforcement agencies, including from authoritarian governments, for surveillance.
- noyb is requesting the Austrian DPA order data deletion, notify all recipients of the complainant's data, impose a fine, and potentially refer the matter to the European Data Protection Board given its broad significance.
