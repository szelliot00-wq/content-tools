# Copyright does more harm than good and should be abolished

Source: https://grapheneos.social/@GrapheneOS/117231186011306184

## Summary
This is a Mastodon thread, not a traditional article. It began as a debate about GrapheneOS's use of AI models for code review, which one user criticized as legitimizing corporate AI and raising concerns about LLM training data licensing. The discussion pivoted when GrapheneOS posted that copyright does more harm than good and should be abolished, arguing it primarily serves as a corporate weapon rather than protecting individual creators.

## Key takeaways
- GrapheneOS argues copyright does far more harm than good and should be abolished, as it fails to protect individuals and small businesses in practice.
- Copyright is characterized as a tool wielded by large corporations to protect monopolies, restrict repair/modification rights, and take down non-infringing content.
- Corporate exceptions to copyright law predate LLMs — the AI debate is just the latest instance of a long-standing pattern.
- One participant (Spark Purcell) partially agreed, but argued copyleft (e.g., GPL-style licensing) is the valid exception worth preserving, as it promotes knowledge sharing and software freedom.
- The thread also touched on LLM training data raising unresolved licensing and consent issues that complicate "open source" software freedom claims.
- Aaron Swartz was cited as an example of copyright law being used harmfully against individuals.
