# Algorithmic rent-pricing litigation expands under new state and local laws

Source: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/algorithmic-rent-pricing-litigation-expands-under-new-state-and-local-laws

## Summary
A new wave of municipal litigation is emerging against multifamily housing landlords who use algorithmic rent-pricing software (primarily RealPage and Yardi), building on existing federal antitrust cases. Cities including San Francisco, San Diego, Seattle, Philadelphia, and Providence have enacted local ordinances banning algorithmic rent coordination tools, and plaintiffs and local governments are now using these laws to pursue follow-on claims with potentially simpler paths to liability than traditional antitrust suits. The article, published by law firm Morgan Lewis in August 2026, warns landlords of escalating exposure and outlines compliance steps.

## Key takeaways
- At least 14 jurisdictions have enacted ordinances restricting algorithmic rent-pricing tools since 2024, with more pending (Montgomery County MD, Evanston IL, Rhode Island).
- Penalties vary significantly by jurisdiction — from $500/day (Providence) to $7,500 per violation (Seattle, King County) — and many laws treat each affected unit and each month of use as a separate violation, creating compounding exposure.
- Municipal ordinances may offer plaintiffs a simpler liability path than antitrust law, since they don't require proving competitive harm — just prohibited software use.
- Prior compliance changes (e.g., federal RealPage settlement restrictions) do not automatically satisfy local ordinances, which use their own definitions of "algorithmic device" and prohibited data.
- The cutoff date when a landlord stopped using prohibited functionality is critical — it determines both liability scope and potential class period in litigation.
- Landlords should immediately audit each property against applicable ordinance effective dates, document when prohibited features were disabled, and assess vendor products against each city's specific definitions rather than assuming federal compliance is sufficient.
- Both private tenant lawsuits and public enforcement actions (like Providence's municipal court case) are being filed, meaning landlords face exposure on two fronts.
