# Thinking fast and slow in AI: The role of metacognition (2021)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01834

## Summary
This 2021 arXiv paper by Ganapini, Campbell, Fabiano, and colleagues applies Daniel Kahneman's "Thinking Fast and Slow" framework to AI architecture design. The authors argue that current AI remains narrow and overly dependent on large datasets and compute, lacking broader human-like intelligence. They propose a multi-agent architecture combining fast "System 1" agents (experience-driven, reactive) with slow "System 2" agents (deliberate, optimization-focused), coordinated through shared world and self-models.

## Key takeaways
- Current AI is narrow and brittle — impressive in specific domains but missing general capabilities that humans consider basic intelligence.
- Kahneman's dual-process theory offers a useful blueprint: System 1 handles familiar problems quickly from past experience; System 2 activates for novel or complex problems requiring deliberate reasoning.
- A multi-agent architecture can implement this split, routing incoming problems to the appropriate agent type based on complexity or confidence.
- Both agent types are grounded in a shared world model (domain knowledge) and a self-model (the system's own history and capability profile) — a form of metacognition.
- Metacognition — the system's awareness of its own knowledge limits and solver skills — is presented as a key missing ingredient in modern AI, not just better algorithms or more data.
