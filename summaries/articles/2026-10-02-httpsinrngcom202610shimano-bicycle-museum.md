# Shimano Bicycle Museum Review

Source: https://inrng.com/2026/10/shimano-bicycle-museum/

## Summary
The Shimano Bicycle Museum, located in Sakai, Japan (~15km south of Osaka), is reviewed as a genuinely impressive permanent collection celebrating the history of bicycles. Despite bearing Shimano's name, the museum prioritizes the bicycle as a machine over corporate promotion, featuring exhibits ranging from early hobby horses to modern race bikes in a spacious, well-designed building. The author visited after a long bike touring trip across Asia.

## Key takeaways
- The museum is bicycle-first, not a corporate showroom — minimal Shimano branding and no gift shop selling Shimano merchandise.
- Exhibits are accessible and hands-on: bikes are not behind glass, allowing visitors to examine details like tubing decals and components up close.
- The collection spans the full history of cycling, including racing bikes (Freddy Maertens' Flandria), Paralympic bikes, mountain bikes, postal service bikes, and a round-the-world touring bike.
- Shimano's own origin story is understated — just a brief mention that its first product was a freewheel, founded by Shozaburo Shimano in Sakai.
- The top floor displays the complete Dura-Ace and XTR groupset history from 1973 to 2021, with empty cases awaiting future editions.
- A research archive with books, brochures, and catalogues makes it valuable for serious cycling historians.
- Access: 30-minute train ride from Osaka; admission is around JPY 500 (under €3), possibly slightly more now.
- Rated as one of the better permanent bicycle museums in the world, comparable to Giant's museum in Taiwan.
