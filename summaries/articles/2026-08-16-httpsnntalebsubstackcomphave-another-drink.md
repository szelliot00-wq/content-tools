# Government sponsored study on alcohol doesn't stand up to scrutiny

Source: https://nntaleb.substack.com/p/have-another-drink

## Summary
Nassim Nicholas Taleb critiques a 2026 US government-commissioned study (George S, Naimi TS, et al.) that claimed alcohol has no protective effect at low doses and carries a 1-in-25 mortality risk at 14 drinks per week. He identifies three core statistical flaws: unexplained asymmetric confidence intervals, improper aggregation of uncertainty across age groups, and over-reliance on the most unreliable data segment (adults over 70) which single-handedly drives the study's conclusions. Taleb argues the paper's methodology is underdocumented and statistically incoherent, making its "definitive" framing unjustified.

## Key takeaways
- The study's confidence intervals are asymmetric in ways that don't match any standard probability distribution, and the supplemental material provides no adequate explanation.
- The total confidence interval reported is tighter than a naive sum of its parts, implying correlated Monte Carlo draws — but the correlation structure is never disclosed or justified.
- The entire mortality claim rests disproportionately on the 70+ age bracket, where standard error is highest, cause-of-death attribution is least reliable, and survivor bias is strongest.
- No sensitivity analysis (e.g., excluding the dominant age bracket or bootstrapping) was performed, which is a serious omission for a paper claiming to overturn longstanding guidelines.
- The study's own Table 1 shows data inconsistent with its headline conclusion, including apparent protective effects of alcohol at low consumption levels.
- The paper's "definitive" framing is not supported by its statistical foundation; Taleb calls it "statistically strange" and underdocumented.
