# Crime Pays but Botany Doesn't

Source: https://www.crimepaysbutbotanydoesnt.com/reading-list

## Summary
This article from the "Crime Pays But Botany Doesn't" blog is a guide for self-taught botany beginners. The author demystifies the field by breaking down two foundational concepts: why scientific Latin naming (nomenclature) exists and how taxonomic classification works. The tone is casual and direct, encouraging curious laypeople not to be intimidated by academic jargon.

## Key takeaways
- Use the internet freely — 24/7 access to information makes self-teaching botany very achievable; look up any unfamiliar term as you go.
- Scientific Latin names exist because common names are unreliable — one common name like "cedar" can refer to many unrelated plants, while a Latin binomial is globally unambiguous.
- Proper scientific nomenclature: genus is capitalized, species is lowercase, and the full name is italicized (e.g., *Cedrus atlantica*) — getting this wrong signals inexperience.
- Modern taxonomy groups plants by evolutionary relationships, which means learning the traits of a family or genus lets you identify unfamiliar plants you've never seen before.
- Recommended foundational texts include *Plant Systematics* by Michael Simpson, *Raven's Biology of Plants*, and *Plant Evolution: An Introduction to the History of Life* by Karl Niklas.
