# ARC-AGI Leaderboard

Source: https://arcprize.org/leaderboard

## Summary

The ARC-AGI Leaderboard tracks AI model performance across three progressively harder benchmarks: ARC-AGI-1, ARC-AGI-2, and ARC-AGI-3. The newest benchmark, ARC-AGI-3, shifts from measuring static "fluid intelligence" to testing AI agents' ability to adapt in real-time to novel interactive environments. The leaderboard visualizes a cost-vs-performance tradeoff, emphasizing that true intelligence requires solving problems efficiently, not just correctly.

## Key takeaways

- **ARC-AGI-3 is dramatically harder than its predecessors** — the top-performing model (Claude Opus 5 High) scores only 30.2%, while the Human Panel scored 98–100% on ARC-AGI-1/2, highlighting a massive gap between AI and human adaptability.
- **Anthropic leads on ARC-AGI-3** — Claude Opus 5 (High, $1.45/task) is the only model to break 10%, with all other AI systems scoring below 8%.
- **Most frontier models score near 0% on ARC-AGI-3** — even high-performing models like GPT-5.6 and Grok 4.5 score under 8%, despite scoring 85–97% on ARC-AGI-1.
- **Older benchmarks are nearly saturated** — many frontier models now approach or match human performance on ARC-AGI-1 and ARC-AGI-2, making them poor discriminators of frontier capability.
- **Cost-efficiency matters** — the leaderboard explicitly tracks cost-per-task, and higher compute budgets (e.g., "Max" vs "Low" settings) yield better scores but at significantly higher cost (e.g., GPT-5.6 Sol Max costs ~$25K total vs $13K for the High tier).
- **ARC-AGI-3 tests a new capability** — the shift to interactive environments means models must dynamically adapt, not just pattern-match, representing a fundamentally harder challenge for current AI architectures.
