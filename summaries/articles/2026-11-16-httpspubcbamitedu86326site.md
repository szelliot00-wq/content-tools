# How to make almost anything

Source: https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/

## Summary
This is the course schedule for "How to Make Almost Anything," a hands-on fabrication course (likely MIT's MAS.863) running from September through December. It covers a broad spectrum of digital fabrication topics, from CAD and electronics to machining and system integration, with weekly lectures and recitations. The course culminates in a final project presentation and open house in mid-December.

## Key takeaways
- The course spans ~15 weeks and covers the full stack of digital fabrication: design, electronics, machining, programming, and mechanical systems.
- Weekly recitations supplement lectures with focused topics like version control, parametric design, debugging, Edge AI, and motor control.
- Core technical areas include computer-aided design/manufacturing, embedded programming, 3D scanning/printing, electronics design and production, input/output devices, and networking.
- The curriculum includes two mid-course reviews (October and November) to assess progress before the final project.
- The course concludes with a final project presentation, a final exam, and a public open house on December 15.
