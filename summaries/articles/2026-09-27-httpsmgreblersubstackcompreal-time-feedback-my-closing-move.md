# Real-time feedback: My closing move in every interview

Source: https://mgrebler.substack.com/p/real-time-feedback-my-closing-move

## Summary
The article by Mark Grebler describes his practice of giving candidates real-time feedback at the end of every interview, transforming the typically one-directional interview into a two-way exchange. Rather than reserving assessments for a post-interview debrief, he shares both positive observations and constructive concerns directly with the candidate, then invites them to respond. This approach benefits both parties: candidates get rare, actionable feedback, and interviewers get a chance to correct any misunderstandings before making a hiring decision.

## Key takeaways
- End interviews by offering real-time feedback — it makes the process two-way and demonstrates company transparency.
- Feedback should be specific and capability-focused (what the candidate did or said), not personality-focused (e.g., "arrogant"), which gives them nothing to act on.
- Allowing candidates to respond can surface genuine misunderstandings that would otherwise lead to a wrong hiring decision — this happens roughly one in five interviews.
- Most candidates are deeply appreciative of any feedback, even critical feedback, because post-interview feedback is so rarely given.
- When a candidate tries to correct a misunderstanding but only reinforces the concern, treat it as a flag to check against earlier interview stages — not a final verdict.
- Negative responses are rare, but when they occur they often reveal something meaningful about culture fit.
- Synthesizing your thoughts quickly and sharing them honestly takes practice and courage, but is worthwhile compared to staying silent until the debrief.
