# “Math 2.0” will need to value mathematical progress more holistically

Source: https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117395269325940185

## Summary
Terence Tao (Fields Medal winner) posted a 4-part thread on Mathstodon arguing that AI is disrupting mathematics by harvesting solutions to open problems without generating the community activity — talks, workshops, collaborations, new researchers — that traditionally follows a breakthrough. He warns this "strip-mining" of open problems is leaving mathematical fields less fertile, and calls for "Math 2.0" to shift focus from raw problem-solving to exposition, community building, and opening new research directions. The thread sparked discussion about entry barriers for non-prodigies, the contamination effect of knowing a solution exists, and broader ethical concerns about generative AI's costs.

## Key takeaways
- **"Math 1.0" over-optimized for firsts**: The field rewarded being first to solve a problem, even without deep understanding — AI has pushed this to an unsustainable extreme.
- **AI-solved problems lack the ecosystem effects**: Unlike traditional breakthroughs, AI-generated proofs rarely produce follow-on seminars, collaborations, or new researchers entering the field.
- **Knowing a solution exists "contaminates" the field**: Even awareness that a problem is solved discourages alternative approaches that might yield deeper insight — and this cannot be undone.
- **"Math 2.0" must value exposition and community**: Future mathematical culture needs to reward explanation, accessibility, mentorship, and opening new questions — not just closing old ones.
- **Academic incentives must be re-evaluated**: Education, publication, and career advancement criteria need explicit revision to reflect the new era.
- **Concern about entry-level researchers**: Respondents worried that AI harvesting "low-hanging fruit" leaves no stepping-stones for emerging mathematicians to build careers.
- **Ethical dissent in the thread**: Some respondents (notably Benoît Régent-Kloeckner) argued that the ecological, economic, and social harms of generative AI make any "responsible use" framing inadequate.
