# Kirlian Photography: Building Your Own Equipment

Source: https://imagesco.com/articles/kirlian/kirlian-photography.html

## Summary
This article from Images Scientific Instruments covers Kirlian photography — a technique that captures electrical coronal discharge around objects on film — and provides hands-on guidance for building your own device. It covers the history and background of the phenomenon, addresses paranormal claims (including the "phantom leaf" experiment), and walks through constructing a basic high-voltage device using an auto-transformer, capacitors, and a discharge plate. It also covers film selection, exposure techniques, and building a transparent discharge plate for visible real-time viewing.

## Key takeaways
- Kirlian photography works by placing an object on a discharge plate connected to a high-voltage source, which causes coronal discharge to expose film directly.
- The core circuit uses a high-voltage auto-transformer (T1) with three wires — two enamel primary wires and one high-voltage output wire — along with capacitors and a switch; polarity of the primary wires does not matter.
- Paranormal claims (such as the "phantom leaf" effect showing a "aura" of a cut leaf) have mundane explanations and are not scientifically validated.
- Sheet film is used instead of a camera, with the object placed directly on the film atop the discharge plate.
- A transparent discharge plate allows real-time observation of the corona discharge rather than relying solely on film exposures.
- Commercial Kirlian devices are available online, but the article emphasizes that a functional device can be built from relatively simple components.
