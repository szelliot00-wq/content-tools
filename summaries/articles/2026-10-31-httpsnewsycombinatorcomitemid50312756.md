# Tell HN: Scribd Breached

Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50312756

## Summary
Scribd, Inc. (parent company of Scribd, Slideshare, Everand, and Fable) disclosed a security breach in which an unauthorized third party accessed user data including email addresses, last 4 digits of credit cards, card issuer, expiry dates, and payment history. Notifications were sent to affected users starting in early October 2026, with staggered rollout continuing through late October. The Hacker News thread generated significant discussion about broader cybersecurity accountability, AI's role in attacks, and the fact that Scribd suffered a nearly identical breach 13 years ago in 2013.

## Key takeaways
- **Data exposed:** Email addresses, last 4 digits of credit card, card issuer, expiry date, and payment history — no full card numbers or passwords confirmed stolen.
- **Staggered notification:** Scribd posted a security notice on October 1, 2026 but rolled out individual email notifications in phases, meaning not all users were notified simultaneously.
- **Repeat offender:** Scribd was breached in 2013 under nearly identical circumstances, raising questions about whether their post-2013 security improvements were sufficient or sustained.
- **Accountability gap:** Commenters noted that companies rarely face meaningful consequences beyond fines, and executives seldom face personal liability for repeated breaches.
- **Encryption at rest is insufficient:** A key technical discussion point — encrypting data at rest doesn't protect against application-layer breaches, since the app must be able to read the data to serve it.
- **Virtual cards recommended:** Using virtual/disposable credit card numbers (e.g., privacy.com) limits exposure in breaches like this.
- **Broader breach wave:** Multiple HN commenters noted a surge of breach disclosures around the same time, with speculation about a common exploit (possibly the Cursor IDE compromise mentioned in thread).
