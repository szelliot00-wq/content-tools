# Negativland, Culture Jamming, and the Art of Making Something New

Source: https://blog.archive.org/2026/09/11/negativland-culture-jamming-and-the-art-of-making-something-new/

## Summary
This Internet Archive blog post profiles Negativland, a California-based experimental multimedia collective active since 1980, known for coining the term "culture jamming" to describe their practice of layering found and original material to critique American culture, media, and corporate power. The article highlights the group's 46-year history, its landmark copyright battle with U2's label in 1991, and its ongoing partnership with the Internet Archive to preserve over 4,000 hours of their freeform radio program *Over The Edge*. The piece promotes their live show at the Internet Archive's San Francisco headquarters on September 16, 2026.

## Key takeaways
- Negativland coined "culture jamming" to describe their audio collage art, which uses found material to critique capitalism, media, and power structures.
- A 1991 copyright lawsuit over their U2 parody record led the group to become vocal advocates for copyright reform, resulting in a book and over 140 lectures on appropriation and fair use.
- The Internet Archive has preserved 4,000+ hours of their weekly radio program *Over The Edge* (airing since 1981), making it freely accessible to the public.
- The band deliberately avoids putting faces on albums to keep focus on the work rather than individual personalities.
- Their audience skews toward self-described outsiders and "oddballs" — people who don't fit in even among non-conformists.
- Being difficult to categorize is something the band views as an artistic achievement, not a marketing liability.
