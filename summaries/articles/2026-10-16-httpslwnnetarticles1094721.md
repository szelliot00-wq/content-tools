# Comparing Chromium Development at Google and Igalia

Source: https://lwn.net/Articles/1094721/

## Summary
Sharon Yang, a Chromium developer who spent six years at Google before joining Igalia, gave a talk at FOSSY 2026 comparing the two companies. She contrasted Google's corporate hierarchy (180,000 employees, wide pay range, promotion-driven culture) with Igalia's employee-owned cooperative structure (180 employees, flat pay, no managers). Despite working in the same codebase with many of the same people, the nature of projects and organizational culture differ significantly between the two.

## Key takeaways
- **Organizational structure**: Google is a traditional hierarchy with managers and wide compensation ranges; Igalia is a flat worker cooperative where all employees are paid equally and vote on company decisions.
- **Project types differ**: Google engineers focus on flashy, promotion-worthy features; Igalia engineers do code-health and tech-debt work that Google staff want done but can't prioritize internally.
- **Access friction for external contributors**: External Chromium contributors like Igalia employees face minor but real barriers — delayed access to build systems, visibility bugs from a tracker migration, and Google Docs not shared publicly.
- **No promotions at Igalia**: The elimination of the promotion ladder removes significant stress, and equal pay removes concerns about pay discrimination based on gender or visa status.
- **Performance monitoring still exists**: Coworkers and mentors collectively monitor performance; flat structure doesn't mean accountability disappears.
- **Scale is an open problem**: At ~180 people, Igalia's flat model works, but everyone acknowledges growth will eventually force a structural change — no clear solution yet exists.
- **Community overlap**: Despite the company switch, Yang maintained relationships with former Google colleagues and continues collaborating with them on the same codebase.
