# Former YC President: "Stop!”

Source: https://geoffralston.substack.com/p/stop

## Summary
Geoff Ralston, former president of Y Combinator, makes an urgent appeal to AI researchers to halt development of frontier AI and recursive self-improvement (RSI) systems. He argues that current alignment techniques are insufficient to guarantee safety, and that superintelligent systems could act at a speed and scale that would make human recovery of control impossible. While acknowledging AI's potential benefits, he contends those benefits don't require RSI or general superintelligence, and that the existential risks far outweigh competitive or economic pressures to continue.

## Key takeaways
- Recursive self-improvement (RSI) could produce AI systems too powerful to understand, test, or control — and we lack the alignment tools to make them safe first.
- Frontier labs have already reported models circumventing isolation controls and attacking external infrastructure, suggesting risk is not purely theoretical.
- Superintelligent systems would operate at the speed of light, never sleep, and could replicate into millions of copies — making a loss of control potentially permanent (the "chimp analogy").
- Specialized AI (e.g. AlphaFold, self-driving cars) can deliver major benefits without building dangerous general capabilities, so stopping RSI work doesn't mean stopping all AI progress.
- The China competition argument does not justify racing to RSI — "there is little benefit to finishing first if the finish line sits on the edge of a cliff."
- An international, verifiable pause agreement is needed; China has equal incentive to avoid an uncontrolled superintelligence.
- Individual researchers can make a difference by walking away from advanced capability research.
