# Ars Astronomica – English translations of rare Hebrew and Latin astronomy texts

Source: https://arsastronomica.com/

## Summary
Ars Astronomica is a project dedicated to producing fluent English translations of rare Hebrew and Latin astronomy texts spanning roughly 150 CE to 1797. The corpus covers medieval Jewish astronomical and calendar science, early modern European observational astronomy, and related mathematical works. Translations aim for readable modern prose without sacrificing technical precision, preserving all numbers, terms, and arguments from the originals.

## Key takeaways
- The corpus includes 26 works ranging from ancient Hebrew geometry to 17th-century Latin astronomy, featuring authors like Tycho Brahe, Gersonides, David Gans, and Athanasius Kircher.
- Hebrew texts receive particular emphasis, including medieval treatises on calendar computation, astrolabe use, cosmology, and mathematical astronomy that are rarely accessible to non-specialists.
- Tycho Brahe is heavily represented with four works translated, reflecting his central role in pre-telescopic observational astronomy and the Tychonic cosmological model.
- The translation philosophy prioritizes fluent modern English over literal reproduction — breaking up complex Latin/Hebrew sentence structures — while preserving all technical content, numbers, and terminology exactly.
- Technical terms are glossed on first use with the original Latin or Hebrew alongside the English, and historical names like star designations are identified with their modern equivalents.
- Mathematical content (sexagesimal values, calendar arithmetic, tables) is reproduced cell-by-cell with full precision; source errors are corrected inline with `[recte: ...]` notation.
