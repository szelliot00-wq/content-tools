# More German than many Germans

Source: https://mertbulan.com/more-german-than-many-germans/

## Summary
Mert Bulan, a Turkish software developer, recounts how a 2017 summer internship in Hamburg transformed his life and led him to permanently settle in Germany. He describes defying the cold, rule-obsessed German stereotype through warm colleagues, a welcoming flatmate, and a merit-based work culture. Over nearly a decade, he built a career, joined a works council, earned citizenship, and came to identify deeply with German values around social equality, trust, and orderly systems.

## Key takeaways
- First impressions of Germany were shaped by outdated Turkish-community stereotypes; direct experience quickly overturned them.
- A supportive flatmate and an inclusive, fun team made the internship "the best summer of his life" and directly led to a full-time job offer.
- German workplace culture felt notably flat and egalitarian — C-suite executives got their own coffee and argued with works council reps, then shared a beer with them at the next company event.
- Social equality outside work impressed him equally: working-class and suited professionals ate the same pizza at the same price in the same restaurant without anyone thinking twice.
- He appreciated Germany's rule-based society not as bureaucratic rigidity but as cognitive relief — clear standards replace guesswork and distribute trust fairly.
- His soft landing (international company, English-speaking environment, competitive salary) meant his positive experience is not universal; immigrants arriving without those advantages meet a harder reality.
- Integration happened naturally because his values already aligned with German ones, not because he deliberately tried to assimilate.
- Even when rules worked against him — a residency application rejected because he was between jobs despite having savings — he ultimately understood the logic and accepted it.
