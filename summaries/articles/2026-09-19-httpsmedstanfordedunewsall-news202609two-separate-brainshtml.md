# Human brain is two separate organs, Stanford Medicine-led research finds

Source: https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2026/09/two-separate-brains.html

## Summary
A Stanford Medicine-led study published in *Nature Neuroscience* (September 18, 2026) overturns centuries of scientific consensus by demonstrating that the human brain is actually two distinct organs with separate evolutionary and developmental origins. The forebrain/midbrain and the hindbrain (brain stem) arise from different progenitor cells during embryonic development and have fundamentally different genetic configurations. This discovery explains why scientists have long struggled to grow hindbrain neurons in the lab — and opens new doors for researching diseases like ALS and spinal muscular atrophy.

## Key takeaways
- The brain has two independent developmental origins: one progenitor cell (expressing gene *Otx2*) forms the forebrain and midbrain; a separate one (expressing gene *Gbx2*) forms the hindbrain. These populations never overlap.
- This explains decades of failed lab attempts to grow hindbrain neurons — researchers were unknowingly trying to convert forebrain progenitors into cells they are genetically incapable of becoming.
- Using this discovery, researchers successfully grew functional human hindbrain motor neurons from stem cells in the lab for the first time.
- The two-brain pattern dates back at least 550 million years and is present in chickens, zebrafish, and even acorn worms — suggesting evolution fused two ancient, separate neural systems into one organ.
- The finding has direct implications for studying and potentially treating ALS, spinal muscular atrophy (a leading cause of infant death), and other brain stem diseases that were previously nearly impossible to study due to lack of accessible tissue.
- An unexpected side benefit: the hindbrain circuits regulate hunger, which is the same pathway targeted by GLP-1 weight-loss drugs like semaglutide.
