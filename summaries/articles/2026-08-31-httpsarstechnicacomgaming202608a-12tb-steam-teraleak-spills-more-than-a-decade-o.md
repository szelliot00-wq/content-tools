# A 12TB Steam "teraleak" spills more than a decade of lost PC gaming history

Source: https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/08/a-12tb-steam-teraleak-spills-more-than-a-decade-of-lost-pc-gaming-history/

## Summary
A massive 12TB collection of Steam content from 2003–2013 is circulating via BitTorrent, leaked from Valve's defunct "Steam2" content distribution servers. The archive contains thousands of game depots, including public release builds and previously unseen pre-release, prototype, and playtest versions of games from Valve and third-party publishers. Notably, the content was apparently accessible via a public API endpoint with no authentication, meaning it was hidden in plain sight rather than obtained through hacking.

## Key takeaways
- The leak covers every game on Steam's old "Steam2" infrastructure from 2003 until its 2013 replacement by the current SteamPipe system.
- Multiple pre-release versions of *Portal 2* have surfaced, containing cut dialogue, scrapped mechanics (adhesive gel, slow-motion guns, circular portals), and a weapon model tied to the unreleased *Half-Life 2: Episode 3*.
- Some data files labeled "ep3" have been found, offering potential new glimpses into the long-cancelled *Half-Life 2: Episode 3*.
- Early betas of *Left 4 Dead 2*, *CS:GO*, and assets for the cancelled camera-based game *F-Stop* are among other discoveries.
- Third-party game betas are also included, covering titles like *Dragon Age: Origins*, *Batman: Arkham Asylum*, and *Spec Ops: The Line*.
- The leak was described by Valve watchers as Valve's own fault — the content sat on a publicly accessible endpoint with no password protection.
- The readme encourages wide mirroring, making a complete takedown by Valve effectively impossible.
