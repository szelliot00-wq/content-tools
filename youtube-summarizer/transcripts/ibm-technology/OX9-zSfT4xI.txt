The appetite for AI across teams seems to be insatiable. New technology comes out, one team gets it to work, then another team, and another, and pretty soon you have an ecosystem of AI solutions that likely isn't well understood or connected. Every one of those choices could have been reasonable when it was made, a powerful model, a commonly used data source, and a popular framework. we're now past the capability and accessibility stages of AI development. As AI moves from pilots to production, we need visibility, governance and control because AI is everywhere. So the question is how do we avoid chaos? As AI grows and AI agents take on more tasks and chaos isn't an exaggeration. AI inferencing occurring in locations where teams have low to zero control. Different departments adopting different models without centralized visibility or oversight. Sensitive data and AI not complying with corporate policy or regulations. Agentic AI, operating with no oversight and governance teams, unable to see what AI exists across enterprise and what it's doing. These are problems sovereign AI is meant to prevent, deploying without giving up control.  Now let's start with what that really means. Most people's first action is to secure their data, then declare sovereignty. That instinct isn't wrong. Data residency is a huge component in sovereignty. We hone in on the data, protecting it where it lives and complying with policy and regulations, which is critical across all industries. But what can easily happen is we can end up putting a very concrete bubble  around our data and leave everything else stranded outside of it. Today you can have full data sovereignty but still not achieve sovereign AI. Sovereign AI means governing the entire lifecycle, from data ingestion to model deployment, to inference,  to monitoring and everything in between. Teams need visibility and control, including where AI inferencing occurs, what AI and models each team is using, how AI systems interact, what risks those systems introduce, what controls and guardrails apply, who is accountable for them, and finally, whether governance is consistently enforced. But let's take a closer look at one of those areas, where AI actually runs, because underneath all AI is infrastructure. Compute, storage, networking, the platform itself, along with security and governance capabilities around it. All AI needs to run somewhere, be operated by someone, and be accessed by many people. And each one of those is a sovereignty decision, whether you make it deliberately or not. In order to have control over your AI, it's up to you to make decisions on where AI runs,  ensuring that you preserve the ability to make these decisions on your own terms. And here's a nuance. I'm not pitching every AI workflow needs to be built and operated entirely in-house. In fact, nobody is fully sovereign. You are likely running a hybrid model with some of your workloads on somebody else's hardware or libraries or models or cloud, and that's totally fine. AI sovereignty is a scale. It's about making these hard decisions on which workloads get which treatment. For example, maybe for a highly sensitive or regulated workload, you need to have all AI and operations be in-house or on premises. Meanwhile, other projects can run on a public cloud, benefiting from scale, cost and flexibility. Sovereignty isn't free, it's a cost that needs to be balanced. You need the flexibility to determine where AI runs based on unique risk, security, compliance, performance, and business requirements. It's inherently not a one solution fits all situation.  You need control over infrastructure, data and AI inference location, model selection and deployment environments. Now I'm not prescribing a specific solution. Sovereignty is about you preserving your ability to make all of these decisions on your own terms, combining flexibility and security at scale. So why do we need this? It may seem easier to simply choose the best technology for each use case and move forward. And it is easier right up until organizations can explain what their AI systems are doing, what they connect to, where the information is, and why. Well, AI systems must be explainable, governed, and auditable. In the same way you are making sovereignty decisions about what external systems to use, your users are looking at you in the same way. In order for someone to trust an AI solution, those requirements become mandatory. But how do we do that? Teams need clearer policies for how models are developed, tested, monitored and updated, in addition to whether these controls are actually being enforced. a policy is static. The AI environment is not, which means rules need to translate into controls that can be continuously enforced as AI operates. Establishing a sovereign boundary around applications, data and AI helps create a foundation  where policies can be enforced consistently across the stack. This gets even more imperative as we consider that AI agents take real actions across real systems on their own. Instead of static AI questioning which models are being used, agents open up questions on what they should be allowed to do, and if we can reconstruct it and audit those independent actions. With agents, our approach to sovereign AI must adapt and incorporate all of these concerns across an enterprise. Now, I've seen this before. Adding gates and approvals in front of AI doesn't automatically stop improper AI usage. When people have deadlines, that friction can push AI usage outside the governed path. What actually works is making the governed path fast, clear, and easy. Teams shouldn't have to choose between innovation and control. When you have sovereign AI, you have a framework where both can coexist. By establishing the right controls, teams can confidently adopt AI, accelerate transformation, and unlock more value from their data. Here's one example of what that may look like. Let's say I provide my teams with a platform of approved models, services, and AI capabilities. When anyone wants to go create a new use case, innovation is simple as users can choose among already governed decisions, as we automatically maintain oversight of what can be deployed and under which conditions. When sovereignty is built in, the rest becomes easy. It's not limiting the number of AI projects, just making sure they all inherit the same controls. As AI becomes increasingly central, sovereignty will become a critical factor in determining which teams can scale AI successfully and responsibly. We started with sovereignty as a bubble protecting our data. The point of AI sovereignty is this bubble needs to be a lot bigger, encompassing models, inference infrastructure and the decisions being made on top of them. You're always going to be running something you didn't build. AI sovereignty just means you have the control, transparency, and flexibility to use AI on your own terms.
