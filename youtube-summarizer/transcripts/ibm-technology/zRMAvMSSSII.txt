Someone raises a concern about an AI making a high stakes decision and someone else says, don't worry, we have a human in the loop and the room relaxes. I wanna ask you something. When we say a human reviewed it, what we usually mean is a person will look at the AI's output before it goes into the world. That sounds good, but here's what I've learned. A human reviewed, it is not the same. As the system is governed. In fact, most of the time, it's the opposite. AI generates an output, a human reviews it, a decision happens, simple, right? Except there's something we almost never ask. What is the human actually seeing when they review that output? When you trust another person's judgment, like when you really trust them, what are you relying on? You rely on their credibility. Are they drawing on real evidence? You rely their reliability. Can you predict their reasoning? You rely upon their dependability. Did you use the right methods for this decision? You rely your self-orientation. We've all worked with someone whose self-orientation was exclusively internal. Everyone is theirs, every mistake is yours. And you rely on their integrity. Are they still the same trustworthy person that you knew yesterday? Credibility maps to transparency. What data was the model trained on? What methodology? Were those validated choices? Reliability maps to explainability. Why did it produce this output for this person today? Dependability maps to observability. This is especially important for agentic AI. Is the system still performing correctly as conditions are changing? Self-orientation maps to algorithm optimization. What was this model actually optimized for and for whom? And integrity? That maps to robustness against adversaries has this model been tampered with? Five properties, five mappings, all five matter. But here's what's also true. The human in the loop brings something no model replicates. The ability to read circumstances, not just data. To recognize when a case file tells one story and a person's life tells another. To ask not just... What does this output say? But what does this decision mean for this person in this life? Social workers, ethicists, historians, counselors, these are the people who were told for decades their expertise wasn't technical enough to matter in technology decisions. Meanwhile, we build systems that affect millions of people's lives and forgot to include anyone who studies people's life. So, when you say you have a human in the loop, ask yourself if you actually have all four. Visibility, authority, the right measurement, feedback loops, or are you counting warm bodies and calling it governance? It's a starting point. But a human who can see the reasoning, stop the process and bring what no model replicates, that is the beginning of one.
