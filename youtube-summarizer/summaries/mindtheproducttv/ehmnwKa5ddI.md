# The new manifesto for product builders - Faith Forster (Chief Product Officer)

Video ID: `ehmnwKa5ddI`

## Summary
Faith Forster (CPO and founder of discovery.com) joins hosts Lily Smith and Randy Silver to discuss the Makers Manifesto — a new cross-disciplinary framework for product builders in the AI era. The manifesto was developed collaboratively with 45 contributors across multiple regions and is modeled loosely on the Agile Manifesto, but deliberately broader in scope: it covers how to create value through products, not just how to run a software development process. The core argument is that AI has fundamentally changed the economics of building, which makes long-standing principles (purpose, learning, accountability, human oversight) more urgent and more actionable than ever before. The video is most relevant to product managers, CPOs, founders, designers, engineers, and anyone involved in building digital products in an AI-accelerated environment.

## Key insights
- **The manifesto emerged from 96 conversations with product leaders** during Faith's beta for discovery.com, where she observed wildly different levels of AI understanding — from fully automated bug-fix pipelines with no human involvement, to people just wanting faster PRDs ("a faster horse").
- **45 contributors globally** helped create it, including product leaders, AI-native business executives, and practitioners from Australia, London, Lisbon, and Hamburg. The group actively tried to include Asia and Africa representation and non-PM disciplines (engineering, design, marketing) but fell short of full diversity.
- **"Maker" vs. "Builder" was a deliberate choice** — "builder" evokes blue-collar construction workers, while "maker" is inclusive of architects, designers, strategists, and anyone involved in creation regardless of title or role.
- **The manifesto has four values and 16 principles**, structured similarly to the Agile Manifesto: (1) Purpose over possibility, (2) Value created over effort spent, (3) Learning loops over launch plans, (4) Human accountability over full automation.
- **Each value includes a "what it's not" section**, which contributors considered equally important — e.g., measuring performance by tokens spent is explicitly called out as missing the point entirely.
- **"Everything may be possible, but not everything is worth building"** is a core principle under Purpose over Possibility. It is a call to maintain focus and a clear reason to exist, even as AI lowers the cost of building dramatically. Surface complexity and maintenance burden still accumulate even when shipping is easy.
- **The definition of "done" has shifted**: previously a feature was done when it went to production; AI changes this so "done" now means adoption and actual value realization by the customer.
- **Learning loops over launch plans** was the most contentious — contributors would say "we already do that" but on examination often didn't. Faith described sitting next to a customer, pushing an update, and asking them to refresh their screen so they could see the change immediately — an illustration of how radically faster the feedback loop can now be.
- **Product market fit is no longer static**: competitors, customer expectations, and products themselves are all changing so rapidly that teams can fall in and out of PMF quickly. It must be treated as a living, ongoing process rather than a destination.
- **Durable advantage no longer comes primarily from feature sets**: it comes from data, relationships, distribution, business models, pricing, and deep customer understanding — things that can't be copied overnight. The analogy used: major record labels lost some leverage when streaming arrived but retained advantages in marketing power and capital.
- **MCP connections were called out specifically** as a coming disruption to data moats — it will become much easier to move data between systems, eroding advantages held by incumbents who relied on being the system of record.
- **Making context explicit** was the single most actionable takeaway from live workshops. Organizations need strategy, goals, and decision-making context codified in a way that both humans and AI agents can use day-to-day — a strategy deck seen once at the start of the year is no longer sufficient.
- **Anyone can now be a product person** without the title: a SME at Autogen (an AI-native business creating complex business proposals) turned out to be their best prompt engineer because proposal-writing requires the same precision in word choice as prompt engineering.
- **The manifesto is available as both a PDF and a Markdown (.md) file** so it can be fed directly into AI systems (Claude, OpenAI, etc.) as a guiding principle for agents. Some teams are already A/B testing the impact of including the manifesto in agent context vs. not.
- **Over 200 downloads and triple-digit signatories** at time of recording. Workshops were held at Leading the Product (Australia), Productized (Lisbon), and Product at Heart (Hamburg), with strong engagement reported at all three.
- **The manifesto is intentionally not meant to be memorized** — it is designed as a reference for building competency frameworks, hiring criteria, product processes, and agent prompts.
- **A more provocative v2 is in progress**: feedback that the current version is "a bit anodyne" has been acknowledged by the working group. A strident, more provocative draft has recently surfaced, and refinement/debate is underway.
- **Feedback is actively sought at makersmanifesto.org**, with online webinars planned for community input on individual principles and values.

## Use cases
- **CPOs and VP Product** evaluating how AI changes their team's operating model, hiring criteria, and ways of working.
- **Founders of AI-native startups** who need a principled framework for deciding what to build vs. what to skip in a low-cost-to-ship environment.
- **Product teams undergoing AI transformation** in larger enterprises who feel lost, anxious, or overwhelmed by the pace of change and need a grounding reference.
- **Cross-functional teams** (including sales, marketing, customer support, and engineering) who are increasingly co-creating product experiences and need shared principles for doing so responsibly.
- **Prompt engineers and AI agent designers** who want a values-based bedrock to include in agent context or system prompts.
- **Educators and coaches** building product management curricula or competency frameworks that reflect the AI era.
- **Organizations codifying strategy context** for use by both human teams and AI agents in day-to-day decision-making.
- **Anyone building a product over a weekend with AI tools** who wants a sanity-check framework for whether what they're building is worth shipping and maintaining.

## Patterns & frameworks

**The Makers Manifesto (4 values, 16 principles)**
A cross-disciplinary framework for product creation in the AI era. Structured like the Agile Manifesto (short, values-led, with subordinate principles) but deliberately broader — covering purpose, ethics, adoption, and accountability, not just development process. Each value has four principles and an explicit "what this is not" counterexample.

**Purpose over Possibility**
A decision-making filter: understand why your product exists and who it serves, then evaluate what AI makes possible *within that lens*. Counterweight to the temptation to build everything that's now cheap to build. Forces teams to maintain focus and a clear reason to exist.

**Value Created over Effort Spent**
Reject proxy metrics (tokens consumed, features shipped, PRDs written) in favor of actual customer and commercial outcomes. Directly counters organizations that measure AI adoption by usage volume rather than impact.

**Learning Loops over Launch Plans**
Reframes "done" as adoption, not production deployment. Encourages continuous, close-proximity iteration with real users rather than big upfront planning cycles. Equivalent to "fail fast" rebranded for executive palatability, with AI removing the excuse of slow iteration.

**Human Accountability over Full Automation**
Even when agents are doing the work — and even when the product's end user is itself an agent — a human must be accountable for material decisions affecting customers, safety, data, and commercial outcomes. Sets a floor for responsible automation.

**Making Context Explicit**
A principle (and the most actionable workshop takeaway) that strategy, goals, and decision criteria must be codified in persistent, accessible form — not just communicated in a quarterly all-hands. Enables both humans and AI agents to make aligned decisions autonomously without constant escalation.

**Durable Advantage Framework**
A mental model for competitive strategy in an era where features can be copied in days: advantage must come from data, relationships, distribution, business model, pricing, and deep customer understanding — not feature differentiation alone. Product-market fit is treated as dynamic rather than a destination to reach and hold.

**Makers as Custodians of Customer Experience**
A role framing: the product function (regardless of who holds it — PM, designer, support, sales) acts as custodian of the end-to-end customer experience. Responsibilities include setting context, enabling others to build within guardrails, and owning final accountability for material changes — analogous to how design systems enable non-designers to apply good design.

**The Design System Analogy**
A mental model for scaling product judgment: just as a design system lets non-designers produce on-brand, high-quality UI, a well-codified product context (goals, principles, constraints) lets non-PMs make good product decisions. Distributes product thinking without requiring a PM in every loop.
