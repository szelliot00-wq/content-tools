# How AI is Changing Product Design (and How to Protect Creativity) | Federico Casalegno (Samsung)

Video ID: `gLTpYdFASrA`

## Summary
Federico Casalegno, EVP and Head of Samsung Design Innovation Centers, discusses how Samsung has built a computational design function that applies AI, machine learning, and 3D/4D scanning data to physical product development — particularly wearables like earbuds, watches, and rings. The core argument is that AI is most valuable as a tool that empowers human designers with real-time, fact-based decisions rather than replacing human creativity or meaning-making. The video is most relevant to product leaders, design directors, and innovation teams at large hardware or consumer electronics companies navigating how to integrate AI into existing design and development processes without losing creative and ethical grounding.

## Key insights
- **Computational design predates the LLM era.** Samsung's AI-driven design practice started in 2018 — well before generative AI interfaces — using ML, 3D/4D scanning, and digital twins for physical product optimization.
- **The four-phase computational design process:** (1) Gather diverse demographic data via 3D/4D scanning, (2) Define KPIs such as fit, comfort, sensor accuracy, (3) Train and validate ML models on that data, (4) Test and iterate across digital, robotic, and real-user environments.
- **Weeks to minutes.** Iteration cycles that previously required physical 3D printing, user testing, redesign, and repeat can now be simulated digitally in minutes, dramatically compressing the design feedback loop.
- **Digital twins + robotics + real users.** Samsung uses all three layers: digital simulation of product variations, robots that mimic human movement (e.g., simulating running with earbuds to test fit dynamics), and final validation with real human testers.
- **Synthetic data is qualitatively curated, not random.** Generative AI is used to expand datasets (e.g., generating more ear or wrist shapes), but only from a base of hand-checked, high-quality data — not raw generative output.
- **Designers remain decision-makers.** The computational team's role is explicitly to surface data and options, not to make calls. Designers retain creative authority; AI provides the facts to inform their judgment.
- **Both convergent (single optimized product) and divergent (customizable variants) outcomes are possible.** For earbuds, one universal form factor can be designed to fit a broader demographic, while accessories like ear tips can be dimensionally varied to cover even more users.
- **AI formula: AI = EI + HI.** Samsung's CDO defines AI as "Emotional Intelligence + Human Imagination" — the tools only matter when combined with empathy and creative human agency.
- **Team size is small but cross-disciplinary.** Federico's teams in San Francisco, Paris, and Korea are 5–15 people each — not large, but deliberately composed of diverse backgrounds: social scientists, engineers, strategists, ethnographers, designers, and creative technologists.
- **The danger of being used by the tools.** A recurring theme: LLMs and AI can easily drive the agenda if teams aren't disciplined. Keeping humans "on a short leash" of AI (rather than the reverse) is critical to preserving meaning.
- **Productivity is solved; meaning is not.** AI can produce a love letter in 2 seconds, but feeling love is different. The bottleneck has shifted from output volume to meaning, relevance, and genuine human impact.
- **Domain expertise is non-negotiable.** Synthetic personas and generated data are only as useful as the domain expertise of the teams interpreting them. Without knowing what you're designing for, the data is noise.
- **Two modes of problem-solving run in parallel:** top-down (specific requests from design/R&D teams) and emergent (novel problems surfaced during the computational design process itself). He calls these "prototyping problems" vs. "prototyping solutions."
- **Team hiring criterion is curiosity and ethical compass, not just technical skill.** In a market where many candidates have similar credentials, what differentiates teams is openness, collaboration style, and values.

## Patterns & frameworks

**Computational Design Process (4-phase)**
A repeatable methodology for applying AI/ML to physical product development:
1. *Data acquisition* — 3D/4D scanning across representative demographics
2. *KPI definition* — what does "fit," "comfort," or "sensor accuracy" actually mean numerically?
3. *Model training* — build and validate ML models on curated data
4. *Test and iterate* — across digital twin, robotic simulation, and real-user testing layers

**Three-Layer Testing Environment**
A validation stack used to compress iteration cycles: (1) digital twin simulation, (2) robotic physical mimicry, (3) real human user testing. Each layer catches different failure modes and can be cycled rapidly.

**AI = EI + HI (Samsung's AI Formula)**
Coined by Samsung's CDO Mauro Porcini: Artificial Intelligence is only meaningful when amplified by Emotional Intelligence (empathy, human understanding) and Human Imagination (creative leaps, vision). Positions AI as an input to human judgment, not a replacement for it.

**Prototyping Problems vs. Prototyping Solutions**
A mental model distinguishing two distinct creative phases: (1) exploratory/generative — understanding and defining the right problem space (requires a designer's mindset), and (2) engineering — solving a well-defined problem with tools and data. Conflating the two leads to building the wrong thing faster.

**Human-Centered North Star**
Not a formal named framework, but an operational principle: the mission is always "understand and improve people's lives" — not to ship products or deploy AI. All tool and process decisions are evaluated against this compass. Prevents tool-chasing and keeps teams grounded when priorities conflict.

**Cross-Disciplinary Small Team Model**
Teams of 5–15 people composed across social science, design, engineering, strategy, and ethnography — united not by shared tools or titles but by curiosity and ethical alignment. Designed to maximize productive friction and collaborative insight while staying agile.
