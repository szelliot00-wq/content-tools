# Payments is about trust, technology, human behaviour and adoption.

Video ID: `I3S_vreS8mU`

## Summary
This episode of the Payments Leader Show features Dean from ACI Worldwide in a wide-ranging conversation about the payments ecosystem. The discussion covers payment sovereignty trends, the slow death of legacy "spaghetti" architectures, lessons from contactless adoption, and the emerging role of AI and agentic payments. A recurring theme is that technology adoption in payments is fundamentally driven by human behavior and trust rather than technical capability.

## Key insights
- **Payments sovereignty is reshaping the competitive landscape.** Countries are increasingly building domestic alternatives to US card schemes (Visa/Mastercard), moving toward direct account-to-account payments — a trend accelerating with current geopolitical tensions.
- **Legacy "spaghetti" architectures are the biggest overlooked risk.** The dangerous assumption is that complex, decades-old infrastructure can keep running indefinitely. Technology has now advanced enough to challenge that, but organizations resist the risk.
- **What's hype in one market is today's reality in another.** Crypto/stablecoin adoption has moved far slower than expected; AI has moved far faster. Market context determines whether something is a signal or noise.
- **Leapfrogging happens where old infrastructure never existed.** India's UPI and Brazil's PIX succeeded by bypassing card infrastructure entirely and going straight to mobile-based account payments — enabled by widespread mobile and 4G penetration.
- **Human behavior, not technology, sets the pace of adoption.** Contactless payments existed for years before COVID and TfL's adoption created the behavioral shift needed for mass uptake. The technology was ready; the humans weren't.
- **Trust is the foundation everything scales on.** No payment innovation achieves mass adoption until consumers trust it — contactless, agentic payments, and stablecoins all follow this pattern.
- **AI is transforming how payments professionals work, not just what they build.** Dean describes cutting strategy development time by 75% using AI, but warns that poor prompting produces "AI goop" — and that training humans to interrogate AI well is itself a fast-moving challenge.
- **Agentic payments have a clear near-term use case but a fuzzy mass-market path.** Simple, low-value, well-defined transactions (cinema tickets) are plausible soon; complex multi-leg travel bookings remain a stretch for mass adoption.
- **Consumers don't know what they want until they see it.** The "faster horses" problem applies directly — payments innovation requires a straw man to react to, not just market research.
- **Cross-industry learning is underused.** Consumer electronics moves faster and provides useful signals for what payment technologies will boom or fade.
