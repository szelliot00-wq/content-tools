# People and mindset matter as much as technology to enable payments to operate effectively

Video ID: `iXZRcEQ_Zx4`

## Summary
This episode of the Payments Leader Show features Denise, co-founder and CEO of Enfuse, a European issuer-processor with over 10 years of growth. The conversation covers what drove Enfuse's expansion, common misconceptions about payments transformation, the human dimensions of fraud, and how culture and values underpin long-term business success. Denise argues that people and mindset — not technology — are the hardest and most critical factors in any payments transformation.

## Key insights
- **Build for scale before you have scale.** Enfuse designed for international scale from day one rather than testing a minimal product, which separated them from competitors optimizing for short-term growth.
- **Technology is the easiest part of transformation; people are the hardest.** The real challenge is getting people — especially long-tenured employees — to rethink entrenched views. You must first get the right people on board, then scale that mindset across the organization.
- **Compliance and innovation are not in tension.** Treating compliance, security, and resilience as add-ons is a mistake. When built into daily operations from the start, they enable rather than obstruct speed.
- **Trust is the currency of payments.** Payments are global but bought locally — proof points and anchor customers in each new market matter more than a strong reputation elsewhere.
- **Fraud has a human cost beyond financial loss.** Enfuse research found women are disproportionately emotionally impacted by fraud — experiencing anxiety, self-blame, and loss of trust in digital payments — at higher rates than men. The industry needs to address fraud as a human issue, not just a cost line.
- **Protect values, not culture.** Culture must evolve as a company grows and diversifies geographically; what must stay fixed are the underlying values (transparency, direct communication, collaboration, long-term thinking) that translate into non-negotiable behaviors.
- **Reading between the lines drives better products.** Enfuse's embedded analytics product emerged not from an explicit customer request but from recognizing that raw data access doesn't equal useful insight — anticipating the next question is more valuable than answering only the one asked.
- **Healthy disagreement is undervalued in leadership.** A leadership team that always agrees is a warning sign. Encouraging opposing views and productive tension leads to better decisions than consensus-seeking.
- **Consumer adoption, not technology, determines payment method success.** Contactless payments existed for years before the pandemic normalized them — how and why the industry introduces technology to consumers matters as much as the technology itself.
- **The "end of cards" has been overpredicted.** Card payments continue to grow faster than open banking in Europe; alternative payment methods will expand but are unlikely to displace cards within the near-term horizon.
