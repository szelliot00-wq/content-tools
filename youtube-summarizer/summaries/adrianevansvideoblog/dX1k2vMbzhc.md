# Keep curiosity alive, innovation is not correlated to fear

Video ID: `dX1k2vMbzhc`

## Summary
This is a podcast interview with Amna, a former payments and fintech executive with a 23-year career across 14 countries at institutions including Citibank, Standard Chartered, and Mastercard, who left corporate life to write *The Vested Leader*. The conversation covers her leadership philosophy centered on choosing people over power, the value of self-reflection, curiosity as a driver of growth, and optimism in the face of uncertainty. She shares personal stories illustrating how believing in people — and listening deeply — compounds into outsized outcomes for individuals, teams, and organizations.

## Key insights
- **People over power is a daily choice, not a one-time decision.** Every small moment — a promotion without a pay raise, an inequitable parental leave policy — is a test of whether a leader serves people or institutional comfort. The cumulative effect compounds over time.
- **Self-reflection requires deliberate space.** In a high-velocity corporate career, genuine reflection is nearly impossible without carving out intentional time; writing the book gave Amna the first real opportunity to revisit past decisions and honestly reassess them.
- **Fear and innovation are negatively correlated.** Leaders paralyzed by uncertainty attract weaker talent and breed pessimism, which kills creative thinking. Optimism is a prerequisite for building the future, not a soft luxury.
- **Believe in people more than they believe in themselves.** A finance professional who thought he could never be a client-facing account manager became the team's best — only because a leader refused to accept his self-imposed ceiling. The lift benefited the individual, the team, and the client.
- **Curiosity is a natural human state that environments destroy.** Living and working across 14 countries kept Amna in a permanent beginner's mindset. She argues we are born curious and that judgment, society, and routine are what kill it — the job is simply to keep it alive.
- **The empty chair principle: actively seek the voice not in the room.** Quiet, high-talent people — disproportionately women — often do all the work and say nothing. Leaders must be intentional about listening, not just broadcasting, and make themselves approachable regardless of hierarchy.
- **Deep listening is itself a form of leadership.** A woman sharing her divorce story needed no advice or action — just to be heard. That 15-minute investment cost nothing and meant everything. Many people are simply waiting for someone to listen.
- **The incoming workforce will force leadership models to evolve.** Gen Z has more options than any previous generation, is purpose-driven, and ranks corporate employment low. Leaders who don't adapt will fail to attract the talent they need to compete.
- **Human connection will intensify in value as AI expands.** As machines become more integrated into daily life, the craving for genuine human interaction grows stronger — and leaders who invest in that connection will have a structural advantage.
- **Being vested in people sometimes means helping them leave.** Amna found a role at another company for a team member whose growth was constrained in her organization — a counterintuitive act most leaders would avoid, but the right thing for that person.
