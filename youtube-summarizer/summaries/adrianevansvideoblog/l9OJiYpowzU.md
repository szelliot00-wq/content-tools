# At a decision point - trust your judgement

Video ID: `l9OJiYpowzU`

## Summary
This episode of the Payments Leader Show features host Adrian Evans interviewing Rob Cain, a retired RAF Air Commodore with 32 years of experience flying fast jets. They explore how decision-making frameworks from military aviation apply to business leadership, covering cognitive overload, trained intuition, preparation, visualization, and building psychologically safe cultures. The conversation draws on neuroscience (Kahneman's dual-process theory, metacognition) and aviation frameworks (aviate-navigate-communicate, OODA loop) to offer practical tools for leaders under pressure.

## Key insights
- **Information overload is the enemy of decisions.** The goal is "good enough" decisions with available data, not perfect decisions with complete data. Sullenberger landing on the Hudson used a single visual rule of thumb, not complex math.
- **Trained intuition is reliable; untrained intuition is not.** Gary Klein's fire chief example illustrates that gut instinct is only trustworthy when backed by years of experience and rich feedback loops.
- **Metacognition — knowing how you're doing while you're doing — is a trainable skill.** Recognizing the "coning of attention" (HUD fixation) is the first step to breaking out of overload.
- **Aviate, navigate, communicate** translates to business as: stabilize yourself first (sleep, health, mindset), remember your vision and direction, then communicate — in that order.
- **Immediate actions vs. subsequent actions** mirrors Kahneman's fast/slow thinking: do something to stabilize the situation first, then slow down for analytical follow-through.
- **Psychological safety is the prerequisite for learning.** A culture where leaders openly admit errors (and gain credibility for it) is the only environment where honest debriefs — and real improvement — can happen.
- **Prepare, perform, review, adapt.** Most organizations do the "perform" step well but skip the review and adapt phases, which are where compounding improvement actually happens.
- **Visualization is roughly 80% as effective as real practice.** The PETTLEP technique requires recreating physical, emotional, and time-pressure conditions mentally — not just imagining the win, but rehearsing the full process in real time.
- **Performance = Potential − Interference** (Tim Gallwey's formula). Self-limiting beliefs and imposter syndrome are the hardest interferences to remove, and removing even one meaningfully raises output.
- **Recursive metacognition improves decision quality by 20–30%.** After reaching a decision, scoring your own confidence in it (and asking AI to do the same) meaningfully improves outcomes, per MIT research on LLMs and Stephen Fleming's work on human cognition.
- **AI amplifies judgment but cannot replace it.** People who offload all thinking to AI without developing their own judgment lose the ability to detect when AI is wrong. Human context, relationship history, and intuition remain irreplaceable.
- **Trust your judgment at the decision point.** There is a moment before which you can gather more information and after which the environment acts on you. Recognizing that point — and committing — is a core senior leadership skill.
- **Consistency is the meta-skill.** Federer won only 54% of points but 80% of matches. Small, consistent marginal gains in preparation, habits, and review compound into elite performance over time.
