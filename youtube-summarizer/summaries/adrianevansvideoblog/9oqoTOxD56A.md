# Trust, curiosity and teamwork remain the foundations of lasting success.

Video ID: `9oqoTOxD56A`

## Summary
This episode of the Payments Leader Show features Kez, Head of Payments at Nord Security (makers of NordVPN), discussing the deep intersection of cybersecurity and payments. The conversation covers fraud prevention, involuntary churn reduction, local payment preferences, and the AI arms race between fraudsters and defenders. Kez closes with leadership lessons for anyone building a career in payments.

## Key insights
- **Identity is the real overlap between cybersecurity and payments.** Every payment is an identity assertion, and every cyberattack targets that same assertion. Concepts like tokenization and 3D Secure are cybersecurity tools applied to payments.
- **Friction comes from poorly implemented security, not security itself.** Challenging a returning subscriber on a saved card is unnecessary friction; prompting a first-time buyer to confirm a billing address is smart risk management.
- **Risk-based authentication is the unlock.** Behavioral signals — device fingerprint, session history, transaction velocity — should route low-risk transactions through seamlessly and trigger challenges only for high-risk ones.
- **Involuntary churn from payment failures can account for 20–40% of total churn** in subscription businesses. Basic retry logic recovers ~15% of failed payments; intelligent retries, card updaters, and multi-processor fallback setups can boost recovery rates 20–50% beyond traditional methods.
- **There is no global checkout — payments is deeply local.** iDEAL dominates the Netherlands (92%), Blik dominates Poland, Pix has 70%+ adoption in Brazil, BNPL is strong in Germany, and digital wallets dominate Asia country by country. Missing a market's preferred method means instant abandonment.
- **The AI fraud fight is an arms race with no clear winner.** Fraudsters now access off-the-shelf dark web tools with minimal technical skill. Defenders hold an asymmetric advantage through the transaction graph — vast data on device fingerprints, behavioral patterns, and velocity signals that attackers cannot see.
- **Sophisticated fraudsters operate like businesses chasing ROI.** They probe for weak defenses, cut losses when defenses are strong, and move on. Closing gaps across an entire organization — not just one product — forces them to leave.
- **The best fraud defense combines in-house knowledge with external vendor network signals.** Vendors like Forter, Riskified, and Sift aggregate cross-merchant data; in-house teams know their own business deeply. The combination is more powerful than either alone.
- **Alternative payment methods (APMs) carry lower fraud risk by design.** Bank-authenticated methods like iDEAL, Pix, and Blik require users to authenticate via their bank app, but most risk models still center on cards — a gap that will need to close as APMs grow.
- **Payments leadership requires breaking silos.** Risk, engineering, product, and finance each optimize their own metrics; a leader's job is to define a shared metric (e.g., LTV) that aligns all teams toward the same goal.
- **Don't outsource your payments thinking to your PSP.** Acquirers optimize for approval rates and fraud prevention at a surface level; deeper metrics — customer experience, false positive rate, long-term revenue — must be owned internally.
- **Curiosity is a leadership superpower in payments.** The field grows more complex every year; those who stay curious and data-literate consistently outperform those who rely on vendors or static processes.
