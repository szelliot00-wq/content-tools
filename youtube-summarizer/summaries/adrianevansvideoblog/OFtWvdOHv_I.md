# The Warning Signs of Redundancy (And What to Do Before It Happens)

Video ID: `OFtWvdOHv_I`

## Summary
Career coach Adrien Evans joins the host to discuss how senior executives can recognize early warning signs of redundancy and prepare proactively rather than reactively. Using the story of a client named John, Evans illustrates how forensic observation of workplace signals — combined with a structured 14-step "executive advantage" process — allowed John to secure three job offers before he was officially let go. The conversation emphasizes owning your career, maintaining an active network, and negotiating from a position of strength rather than desperation.

## Key insights
- **Trust your instincts early.** Subtle shifts — being excluded from meetings, side conversations you're not part of, a change in tone from leadership — are often the first signals that something is coming.
- **Micromanagement and sudden KPI pressure are red flags.** When a company starts measuring you more intensely than before, especially in roles with long sales cycles where weekly targets don't make sense, it often signals an exit is being engineered.
- **Structural events accelerate risk.** Mergers, acquisitions, and funding rounds you're suddenly excluded from are strong indicators your role may be eliminated.
- **Six months is enough time to flip the situation.** John went from being warned to having three job offers before the redundancy meeting even happened — preparation converts a crisis into leverage.
- **The redundancy meeting itself becomes low-stakes when you're prepared.** John's strategy: listen, say nothing, ask for everything in writing, leave. The CEO was visibly thrown by his composure.
- **Negotiate the exit package as a first offer, not a final one.** Treat the initial package as an opening bid and work with advisers to counter — this is especially powerful when you already have other offers.
- **Own your career actively, not reactively.** Your employer is not responsible for your career trajectory; you are. Plan in cycles (like Olympians) with clarity about where you want to go next.
- **Network continuously, not transactionally.** Reaching out to people only when you need something feels "icky" — and it is. John secured seven interviews from seven calls because he had maintained those relationships over time.
- **The goal is antifragility, not just resilience.** Resilience means bouncing back to where you were; antifragility means coming out stronger than before the disruption.
- **Flow state signals meaningful work.** Evans defines peak engagement as the intersection of high skill and high challenge — a useful personal benchmark for evaluating whether a new role is worth taking.
