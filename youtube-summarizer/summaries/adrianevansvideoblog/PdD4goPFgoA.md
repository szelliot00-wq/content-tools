# Your career will be jolted in the next 15 months - give yourself The Executive Advantage

Video ID: `PdD4goPFgoA`

## Summary
Adrian (a career adviser for senior executives) and host Matt discuss "The Executive Advantage," a career preparation system aimed at helping executives anticipate and navigate career disruptions ("jolts") before they happen. Drawing on personal experiences — including his father's stroke at age 50 and witnessing a brutal executive redundancy — Adrian argues that proactive career management, strategic relationship-building, and an antifragile mindset are essential for long-term career resilience. The conversation covers his five-stage methodology, the concept of "next next" planning, the value of sponsors, and why performance alone is never enough in organizational politics.

## Key insights
- **Career jolts are inevitable within 15 months** — Adrian states this not as alarmism but as data-driven fact given today's volatile business environment; the only question is whether you're prepared when it happens.
- **Antifragile beats resilient** — Resilience means bouncing back; antifragility means actively embracing disruption and emerging in a better position than before, not just returning to the prior state.
- **Pattern recognition is a competitive advantage** — An outside adviser can spot signs of impending redundancy (sidelining from meetings, M&A activity, shifting stakeholder behavior) that the executive, too absorbed in day-to-day work, cannot see themselves.
- **"Next next" planning** — Borrowed from Olympic athletes who plan across four-year cycles, the idea is to always know your next career destination and the one after that, so daily decisions can be evaluated against a long-term trajectory.
- **Performance is necessary but insufficient** — Organizational politics and sponsorship (powerful people who advocate for you when you're not in the room) matter more than raw performance when it comes to promotion and career advancement.
- **Relationships are the compounding asset** — Every resource on the planet is held by people; investing in a high-quality, reciprocal network over years (paying it forward before expecting returns) is what makes career success "inevitable."
- **Career management as an insurance policy** — Just as you insure your home before disaster strikes, you should invest in career strategy when things are going well — not after the jolt has already landed and self-esteem and income are already damaged.
- **Zone of genius over generalism** — Identify the five things you do better than almost anyone else and ruthlessly focus there; outsource or delegate everything else rather than wasting energy on your "Z-game."
- **Mindset compounds alongside skillset** — A growth mindset (Carol Dweck's "I can't do that *yet*") is as valuable as technical expertise, and belief in oneself in high-stakes moments is what separates equally talented people.
- **AI enhances but doesn't replace human connection** — The framework for using AI productively: define the project, brief the AI, let it do the heavy lifting, evaluate and iterate, then return decision-making to the human. Human trust and connection remain irreplaceable.
