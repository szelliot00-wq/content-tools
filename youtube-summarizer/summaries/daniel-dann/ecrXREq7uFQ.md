## Patterns & frameworks
**Mobile data vs. local line distinction**
A core mental model Daniel repeats throughout: connectivity (being online) and identity (having a verifiable local contact) are separate layers. A travel eSIM solves layer one; a local phone number subscription solves layer two. Applying this framework helps travelers diagnose *why* they're being blocked — it's not a connectivity issue, it's an identity/verification issue.

**Pre-purchase transparency checklist**
Daniel implicitly endorses evaluating a service by how clearly it communicates requirements before payment. The checklist is: (1) confirm device compatibility, (2) understand what triggers identity verification, (3) review the full order breakdown (line fee + allowance fee + tax + credits) before entering payment. Saily's design surfaces all of these before checkout, which he frames as a quality signal.

**Right-size your allowance**
Rather than defaulting to the largest plan, Daniel proposes a use-case-first approach: define *why* you need the line (verification texts only vs. actual conversations), then match the allowance to realistic 30-day usage. Small allowance = verification and occasional SMS; larger allowance = active voice use.
