# 5 Best Email Clients for Teams (2027) - One Might SURPRISE You

Video ID: `jy3O8pDDNKw`

## Summary
Daniel reviews five team email clients — Missive, Front, Spark, Shortwave, and Superhuman — by running each through the same workflow focused on shared inboxes, automation, and AI. The core argument is that team email breaks down when multiple people handle the same customer conversation without clear ownership or coordination. The video is most relevant to small-to-medium teams dealing with shared customer communication, support workflows, or any context where email responsibility needs to be explicit.

## Key insights
- **Missive (S tier):** Assigns conversations to specific teammates, supports internal comments visible only to the team, and lets each person manage their own read/unread status independently. Supports task creation linked to emails, automation for routing/classification, MCP integration in both directions, and bring-your-own-AI-key with no markup.
- **Front (A tier):** Offers similar assignment and internal comment features with stronger structural rules for routing. Notable weakness: uses forwarding-only email provider integration, so replies or archives made in Front do not sync back to Gmail/Outlook — unlike Missive's two-way sync. Also significantly more expensive than Missive.
- **Spark (B tier):** Retains the feel of a traditional email client while adding collaboration features — assignment, private comments, teammate mentions, co-editing drafts, and shared templates. Downgraded due to reported reliability and syncing issues from users.
- **Shortwave (B tier):** Differentiates on AI — the assistant can summarize threads and help draft replies from within the inbox, and teams can share AI-powered snippets and prompts. Considered more specialized; the advantage diminishes if AI isn't central to the team's workflow.
- **Superhuman (B tier):** Focused on individual speed rather than a unified shared workflow. Includes AI thread summarization, draft assistance, internal discussion, shared snippets, and live "someone is already replying" indicators. Better suited to fast-moving individuals than teams operating from one shared queue.
- **Two-way sync matters:** The Front vs. Missive comparison surfaces a practical risk — if you ever leave a platform that only uses forwarding integration, your email history and state won't be reflected in your underlying provider.
- **Disclosure:** Missive is the video's sponsor, though Daniel states all tests are real and conclusions are his own.

## Use cases
- **Customer support teams** handling a shared inbox where multiple agents might respond to the same thread
- **Small teams without dedicated support tooling** who want collaboration inside their existing email workflow
- **Teams evaluating cost vs. structure tradeoff** — Front for formal process-heavy orgs, Missive for leaner teams
- **AI-forward teams** who want AI assistance embedded in the inbox rather than context-switching to a separate tool (Shortwave)
- **High-velocity individual contributors** on a team who mostly own their own inbox but need occasional coordination (Superhuman)
- **Teams worried about vendor lock-in** who need bidirectional sync with Gmail or Outlook (favors Missive over Front)

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Shared inbox ownership model:** Assign a conversation to one person so responsibility is explicit, eliminating the "who's handling this?" problem. Missive and Front both implement this; it's the foundational pattern the video tests against.
- **Internal comment layer:** Keeping team discussion separate from customer-facing replies — a consistent feature across all five tools, treated as table stakes for team email.
- **Bring-your-own-AI (BYOAI):** Missive's MCP integration allowing users to supply their own AI key without a platform markup — a cost and flexibility pattern worth noting for teams with existing AI infrastructure.
- **Tier list evaluation framework:** Daniel rates each tool on a single consistent workflow (shared inbox + automation + AI) rather than feature lists, then slots each into S/A/B tiers based on how completely it solves the core coordination problem.
- **Specialization spectrum:** The video implicitly frames the five tools on a spectrum from "unified team workflow" (Missive, Front) to "AI-specialized" (Shortwave) to "individual speed with team add-ons" (Superhuman) — useful for matching tool choice to team operating style.
