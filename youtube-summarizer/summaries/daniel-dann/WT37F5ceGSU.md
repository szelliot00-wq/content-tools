# Best Free AI Humanizer (2026) I Tested 3 Free Tools - The Results Surprised Me

Video ID: `WT37F5ceGSU`

## Summary
Daniel tests three free AI humanizer tools — Walter Writes AI, QuillBot, and Grammarly — by running the same ChatGPT-generated essay through each one and checking results against two external AI detectors (ZeroGPT and Scribbr). The video is sponsored by Walter Writes, which ultimately wins the comparison. It is most relevant to students, content creators, or writers who want to make AI-generated text pass detection tools without paying for premium software.

## Key insights
- **Test methodology**: A single ~220-word ChatGPT essay about students using AI for homework was used as the baseline for all three tools, with outputs checked against both ZeroGPT and Scribbr
- **Walter Writes AI (S-tier)**: Rewrote the 220-word essay into 285 words; scored 0% AI-generated on both ZeroGPT and Scribbr — the strongest result of the three tools
- **Walter Writes free "Lite" version**: Separate from the paid trial; supports up to 3,000 words with no account required, making it accessible for longer content without sign-up
- **QuillBot (B-tier)**: Free version is capped at 100 words, forcing the test to use a shortened essay; ZeroGPT flagged the output as 100% AI-generated, while Scribbr estimated 54% AI-generated — inconsistent and weak results
- **Grammarly (A-tier)**: Takes a suggestion-by-suggestion editing approach rather than full rewrites, giving users more editorial control; ZeroGPT scored it 34.3% AI-generated, while Scribbr scored it 0% — a split result
- **Detector disagreement**: ZeroGPT and Scribbr frequently returned different scores for the same text, highlighting that no single detector is authoritative
- **Disclosure**: The video is explicitly sponsored by Walter Writes, which is worth noting when interpreting the rankings
- **Ranking criteria**: Three factors were used — detector results, readability, and free access limits

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Same-input comparison testing**: All three tools were given identical source material, controlling for content variation — a basic but effective comparative evaluation pattern
- **Dual-detector verification**: Each output was checked against two independent detectors (ZeroGPT and Scribbr) rather than one, surfacing how unreliable any single detector score can be
- **Tiered ranking (S / A / B)**: Results were synthesized into a tier list weighted across three dimensions — detector bypass performance, readability/control, and free access generosity — rather than a single numeric score
- **Rewrite vs. suggestion model**: The video implicitly frames two distinct humanization approaches: full automated rewrite (Walter Writes, QuillBot) vs. iterative suggestion-based editing (Grammarly), with the latter offering more user control but less consistent detector bypass
