# 5 Best Apps for Kids to Learn Math in 2026 (Here’s the Winner)

Video ID: `Ts58yZUDwBI`

## Summary
This video reviews five math learning apps for kids — Brilliant, Khan Academy, IXL, Prodigy, and Photomath — comparing their teaching approaches, practice methods, and target learner types. The host, Daniel, argues that the core distinction between useful and not useful apps is whether they help children *understand* how to reach an answer versus simply providing one. Each app is demonstrated using a fractions problem as a common thread, then ranked in a tier system. The video is most relevant to parents choosing a supplemental math tool for elementary-age children (focus on grade 5 content), and it is sponsored by Brilliant.

## Key insights
- **The key evaluation criterion** is whether an app builds understanding or just delivers answers — the host frames this as the central question from the opening.
- **Brilliant (S tier):** Uses an AI tutor named Koji that responds to wrong answers by guiding students through the reasoning rather than revealing the solution. Example: when a fractions attempt is wrong, Koji asks what the student already knows and walks through what 1/2 means conceptually.
- **Khan Academy (A tier):** Offers a broad, school-aligned library with a daily mission, mastery tracking, and gem challenges. Includes TutorMe for step-by-step problem walkthroughs (e.g., common denominators with visual support). Strong for covering subjects a child is already studying in school.
- **IXL (A tier):** Organizes content by grade and specific skill (e.g., grade 5: whole numbers, multiplication, fractions, money, measurement). When a student answers incorrectly, IXL explains the specific error — e.g., showing how each number should be rounded before combining. Also links skills to instructional videos.
- **Prodigy (B tier):** Embeds math problems inside a role-playing game loop — students answer questions to refill "magic" during battles. The math is contextually tied to in-game actions, giving children intrinsic motivation to keep solving problems. Best for engagement-driven learners.
- **Photomath (B tier):** Uses the phone camera to recognize handwritten problems, then provides step-by-step solving stages, explanations of concepts (e.g., how a common denominator works), and animated tutorials. Best as a problem-specific reference tool rather than a full curriculum.
- **Tier summary:** S — Brilliant; A — Khan Academy, IXL; B — Prodigy, Photomath.
- **Sponsored recommendation:** Brilliant offers a free starting tier; premium unlocks full tutor support and all courses; the host's link provides 20% off an annual subscription.

## Use cases
- **Parent selecting a primary math learning app** for a child who struggles with concept comprehension, not just practice volume → Brilliant
- **Parent looking for curriculum-aligned supplemental support** that mirrors what a child is learning in school → Khan Academy
- **Parent or teacher wanting detailed diagnostic practice** with skill-level granularity and error explanations → IXL
- **Parent of a reluctant math learner** who disengages from traditional homework formats → Prodigy
- **Student who needs homework help on a specific problem right now** and wants to see the reasoning, not just the answer → Photomath
- **Homeschooling families** needing a broad, structured resource with mastery tracking → Khan Academy or IXL
- **Children who are already comfortable with basics** but need guided challenge and concept deepening → Brilliant

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Answer vs. Understanding distinction:** The video's core mental model — an app that gives answers without building understanding is less valuable than one that walks students through the reasoning. Applied as the primary filter when evaluating all five apps.
- **Teach → Practice → Feedback loop:** Each app is implicitly evaluated on three dimensions: (1) how it introduces concepts, (2) how it structures practice, and (3) what feedback it gives when a student is wrong. This three-part lens is used consistently across all five demos.
- **Tiered ranking (S/A/B):** A comparative framework used to rank apps not just by quality but by fit — S tier for best overall teaching approach, A tier for strong but narrower utility, B tier for situationally useful tools.
- **Game loop integration (Prodigy-specific):** A pattern where academic tasks are embedded inside game mechanics (e.g., answer math to refill magic) so that the educational activity becomes a means to a game goal, sustaining motivation through intrinsic reward rather than external pressure.
- **Guided error recovery (Brilliant/IXL):** A responsive feedback pattern where wrong answers trigger contextual guidance rather than a simple "incorrect" message — either an AI tutor walking through the concept (Brilliant) or a written explanation of the specific mistake made (IXL).
