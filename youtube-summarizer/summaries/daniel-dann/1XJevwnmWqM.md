## Patterns & frameworks

**Reference-Guided Scene Chaining**
Load a consistent set of visual references (character design, environment) before generating each scene. Rather than re-describing the world in text each time, the same reference materials act as a visual anchor across shots. This is the core repeatable pattern: *same references + new scene-specific prompt = visual continuity*.

**Three-Act Mini-Film Structure**
Daniel applies a compressed storytelling arc across just three clips: (1) Establish the world and character, (2) Introduce a point of tension or mystery, (3) Deliver an emotional resolution. Each scene shifts the story beat while the visual world stays consistent — a practical template for AI short films.

**Incremental Scene Adjustment**
Rather than batch-generating all scenes at once and accepting them, the workflow involves reviewing and adjusting each scene individually before moving to the next. This keeps the director in control of story pacing and visual direction at each step.

**Platform-as-Ecosystem Model**
The implicit framework Akool promotes is consolidating multiple AI engines (video, audio, image) under one interface, reducing the friction of switching between tools. For creators, this means one workflow can span different content types without re-learning separate platforms.
