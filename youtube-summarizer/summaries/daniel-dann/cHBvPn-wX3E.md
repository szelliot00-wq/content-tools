## Key insights
- Koji is embedded directly in the lesson interface — it appears in the lower corner and follows the current material, so learners never have to leave the activity to get help.
- When a wrong answer is submitted, Brilliant highlights the specific parts that caused the error rather than just marking it incorrect, reframing mistakes as part of the learning process.
- Koji responds to mistakes by asking the learner *why* they think the answer was wrong before offering a targeted hint — e.g., "How many blocks does the truck still need to travel north?" — narrowing the problem without solving it.
- A deliberate design choice: Brilliant can ask learners to *explain why a correct answer works* even after getting it right, which guards against lucky guesses passing as understanding.
- Courses use visual, interactive challenges (e.g., arranging command blocks to route a self-driving truck, reordering commands to guide a robot to a gem) rather than lectures or passive reading.
- Difficulty scales gradually — each step builds on the previous one, giving learners time to build confidence before tasks become harder.
- The platform includes streaks, XP, and leagues for light gamification and consistency, but these are kept separate from the core material — rewards only appear after correctly solving challenges.
- Each exercise is connected back to a broader curriculum concept (e.g., the truck routing activity explicitly ties back to sequencing, and previews upcoming topics like loops, logic, and variables).
- Brilliant is positioned as a supplement to school, not a replacement — its value is structured independent practice at the learner's own pace.

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Socratic hinting (Koji's core method):** When a learner is stuck or wrong, Koji asks a guiding question ("Why do you think that was wrong?") and then narrows attention to a specific sub-problem. It never completes the challenge for the learner — the goal is the minimum hint needed to unblock forward progress.
- **Immediate visual feedback loop:** Every challenge shows the result of the learner's input in real time (e.g., the truck drives the route the learner programmed). This closes the gap between action and consequence, making abstract concepts concrete.
- **Graduated difficulty scaffolding:** Lessons are broken into short steps that build incrementally. No step introduces too much new information at once, allowing confidence to accumulate before complexity increases.
- **Post-correct-answer comprehension check:** Even after a right answer, the platform may prompt the learner to explain their reasoning. This pattern catches surface-level pattern matching and reinforces deeper understanding.
- **Gamification as a consistency layer (not a core mechanic):** Streaks, XP, and leagues are additive motivators that reward regular engagement but are structurally decoupled from the learning content itself — you earn them by doing the work, not instead of it.
