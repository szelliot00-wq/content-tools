# 5 Best Smart Tutor Apps in 2026 (for Math and Science)

Video ID: `a9ZxSfKmyJQ`

## Summary
Daniel reviews five smart tutoring apps for math and coding in 2026, comparing how each approaches learning support — from guided problem-solving to raw computation. The core argument is that there's a meaningful difference between tools that hand you an answer and tools that help you understand how to reach it. The video concludes with a sponsored recommendation for Brilliant as the most complete learning experience. It's most relevant to students in grades 5–12, self-learners, and parents looking for supplemental academic support.

## Key insights
- **Brilliant** uses interactive, game-like lessons where learners manipulate visual elements (e.g., arranging squares and rectangles to build a quadratic equation) rather than passively reading. Its built-in AI tutor Koji guides users toward lessons and connects concepts like the distributive property to hands-on steps.
- **Khan Migo** (Khan Academy's AI tutor) refuses to solve problems outright — it asks where the student should begin and guides them step by step. It offers adjustable explanation styles (simpler words, specific term definitions) and can generate visual graphs of functions on request.
- **Photomath** is designed for single-problem, quick-turnaround help. It uses the phone camera to capture a written or printed math problem and returns a step-by-step solution — useful when you need to understand one specific exercise, not a full lesson.
- **Wolfram Alpha** operates at a different tier — it handles calculus, statistics, and differential equations with computational depth, including natural language input (e.g., "solve a second-order differential equation"). It returns results, graphs, and step-by-step breakdowns, but its role is computational rather than instructional.
- **ChatGPT in Study Mode** starts by asking what the student is studying and their level, then breaks topics into smaller parts and checks comprehension as it goes. It uses analogies (e.g., recursion explained as nesting dolls) and asks predictive questions ("what happens first when a function calls itself?") before explaining — covering both math and coding.
- The video distinguishes five distinct support types: academic guidance (Khan Migo), quick visual problem-solving (Photomath), advanced computation (Wolfram Alpha), flexible multi-subject assistance (ChatGPT), and structured interactive learning (Brilliant).
- Brilliant is the sponsored app; viewers get a free trial and 20% off an annual subscription via the description link.

## Use cases
- **Grades 5–12 students** needing supplemental practice outside class, especially for building problem-solving intuition (Brilliant).
- **Students working through homework** who want step-by-step guidance without being handed the answer (Khan Migo, Brilliant).
- **Students stuck on a single problem** who need a fast, visual walkthrough without a full lesson (Photomath).
- **College students or advanced learners** tackling calculus, differential equations, or statistics where computational power matters more than tutoring tone (Wolfram Alpha).
- **Self-learners studying coding** (functions, loops, recursion) who want an adaptive, conversational study partner (ChatGPT Study Mode).
- **Parents** looking for a structured, gamified math and coding curriculum for kids with built-in AI support.
- **Anyone who has previously wasted time searching through videos** for an explanation that fits — these apps provide on-demand, personalized explanations instead.

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Answer vs. Understanding distinction** — The video's central framework: a tool that gives you the answer is categorically different from one that helps you understand the path to it. This is used to rank and differentiate all five apps, with Brilliant positioned at the "understanding" end and Photomath closer to the "answer" end (though it does show steps).
- **Guided Socratic tutoring model** — Used by Khan Migo and ChatGPT Study Mode: instead of explaining first, the tutor asks the student what they think the first step is, forcing active recall and prediction before delivering instruction. This mirrors the Socratic method applied to AI tutoring.
- **Active manipulation learning** — Brilliant's approach of having students physically drag, arrange, or interact with visual elements (rectangles forming a quadratic) rather than reading or watching. The framework is: learn by doing the math, not observing it.
- **Tiered complexity matching** — The video implicitly organizes the five tools on a complexity axis: Photomath (single problems) → Brilliant/Khan Migo (structured curriculum) → ChatGPT (multi-subject, adaptive) → Wolfram Alpha (graduate-level computation). Choosing the right tool means matching tool depth to learner need.
- **Adjustable explanation scaffolding** (Khan Migo) — A specific feature pattern where the learner can request simpler language, term definitions, or visual representations mid-explanation, allowing the same tool to serve different comprehension levels dynamically.
