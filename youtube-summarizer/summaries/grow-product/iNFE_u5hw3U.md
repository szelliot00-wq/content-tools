# The New AI PM Interview Round Where You Build Live, Not Just Talk

Video ID: `iNFE_u5hw3U`

## Summary
This video demonstrates a live mock "vibe coding" interview — a hybrid PM interview format that combines traditional product sense with hands-on AI prototyping. The host (Akash) walks through a 45-minute mock session where he must design and prototype a LinkedIn feature to help users reconnect with weak network ties, using AI tools in real time. The core argument is that this emerging interview format rewards PMs who can move quickly through user/problem framing and then demonstrate genuine AI-native prototyping fluency across multiple tools in parallel. It is most relevant to PMs targeting AI-focused roles at companies like Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Uber, and Atlassian, where compensation ranges from $300K to over $1M.

## Key insights
- **The "vibe coding round" is a new hybrid interview format** — not a traditional product sense round, not an engineering coding round, but a combination where you design a feature and build a live prototype in ~30 minutes.
- **AI PM roles at top companies pay $300K–$1M+**, making this interview format high-stakes and worth dedicated preparation.
- **Existing product features, not greenfield products, are the most common prompt.** Most online content focuses on building from scratch, but interviewers are more likely to ask you to evolve an existing product within its constraints.
- **The 20-60-20 rule for AI usage:** The first 20% (initial thinking) and last 20% (final refinement) should still be done by a human. AI handles the middle 60%.
- **Speed-running the product sense portion is critical.** Akash spent ~5–6 minutes on what would normally be a 30-minute product sense interview, deliberately trading depth for time so the prototyping phase gets enough runway.
- **User segmentation with numbers matters.** Akash bucketed LinkedIn users: ~99% content consumers vs. 1% content creators; ~95% don't engage their network vs. 5% who do. Within non-engagers: ~5% actively job searching, ~80% soft/passive job searching, ~15% not career-focused.
- **Focus users: soft job searchers** — people who want to maintain their professional profile and warm their network without an immediate job search need.
- **Two core problems identified via live product audit:**
  1. Poor recommendation of *who* to reach out to (broken "Catch-up" feature, broken "Grow Your Network" button, notification coupling that hides content behind in-app notification settings).
  2. Poor *context* for cold outreach — the existing "Write with AI" message feature doesn't pull in relationship history or shared context, and produces generic, un-humanized text (noted: excessive em-dashes).
- **The live product audit was rated as better than 95% of candidates.** Opening the actual app during an interview rather than theorizing is a significant differentiator.
- **A "harness" in Claude Code gives you a head start.** Before prompting, Akash set up three foundational assets in a blank Claude Code folder: a design system (from screenshots), a context/strategy library, and a prototyping skill.
- **Parallel prototyping across 3+ tools simultaneously** is a key signal of AI-nativeness. Akash ran Claude Code, Magic Patterns, and Lovable concurrently to avoid dead time while models generated output.
- **Good prompts have four elements:** (1) clear steps of the task, (2) defined context, (3) explicit output format/requirements, (4) stated non-goals/what you don't want.
- **Tool selection by task type:**
  - Magic Patterns: frontend-only, fastest output, no backend
  - Lovable: frontend, can publish to GitHub for handoff
  - Claude Code (Fable 5): full harness, design system awareness, concept generation
  - Cursor + Claude/Codex: IDE-based backend work
  - Codex: long-running backend tasks, cheaper, better as a "workhorse"
  - Fable 5: preferred for frontend taste and shorter creative bursts; Sonnet for prompt generation
- **Claude's Fable model was specifically praised** for adhering to the LinkedIn design system and incorporating the "warmth/cold outreach" direction from the PRD.
- **Five concepts generated by Claude Code:** Warm Threads (homepage warm signal surfacing), Reconnect Queue (dedicated inbox-style tab), Network Constellation (visual relationship heat map), Profile Context Layer (contextual overlay on profiles), Home Moments (notification-style triggers). Final recommendation: Warm Threads + Reconnect Queue + Drafts, with the Constellation idea being less practical than it seemed conceptually.
- **PRD non-negotiables in this format:** (1) Hypothesis/goal tied to business mission, (2) Non-goals (no spam, no out-of-context messages), (3) Leading metrics (message send rate, response rate) and lagging metrics (retention).
- **Weak connection definition for PRD:** First-degree connections with no messages or content engagement in the last 3 months. Context signals: shared employers, academic institutions, locations, engagement history, application/interest data.
- **Post-prototype workflow:** Internal user test → UserTesting.com or UserVoice for paid user feedback → backend build → connect to real codebase/design system → 0.1% A/B test.
- **Scoring breakdown (interviewer's assessment, ~8.5–9 overall):**
  - User: 8.5–9 (strong segmentation, mission anchoring)
  - Problem: 8 (needed a push to get to cognitive load/hurdle framing faster; live audit was the highlight)
  - Solution: 8.5–9 (clear directional concepts; could use more opinion/specificity)
  - PRD: 8–8.5 (clean goals, non-goals, leading/lagging metrics)
  - Prompt: 9–9.5 (AI-generated prompt still valid; ability to explain what makes a good prompt was key)
  - Parallel tooling: high marks (demonstrated tool selection rationale and concurrent workflow)
  - Backend: 8 (verbal walkthrough was sufficient signal given time constraints)
- **Iterating on an existing product is harder than greenfield** because constraints and assumptions are already baked in — interviewers specifically use this to test whether candidates surface and respect those constraints.

## Patterns & frameworks

**UPS PPPB Framework (the vibe coding interview structure)**
The overarching framework Akash uses and teaches. Stands for: **U**ser → **P**roblem → **S**olution → **P**RD → **P**rompt → **P**arallel prototyping → **B**ackend. Each stage has a rough time allocation (flexible based on interviewer cues), and the key discipline is moving quickly through U/P/S to preserve time for the prototyping phases. Backend often gets cut to 10–15 minutes or skipped if time runs short.

**The 20-60-20 AI Collaboration Rule**
The first 20% of work (initial thinking, framing, user segmentation) should be done by the human before touching AI tools. AI handles the middle 60% (generation, prototyping, prompt drafting). The final 20% (refinement, taste, evaluation) returns to the human. This prevents AI from producing undirected or shallow output.

**Parallel Prototyping Workflow**
Rather than iterating sequentially on one tool, run 3+ AI prototyping tools simultaneously (Claude Code, Magic Patterns, Lovable). Each tool runs its generation in parallel, and you evaluate outputs across all of them to pick the strongest concept to iterate on. This maximizes output in the time-compressed interview setting and signals AI-nativeness.

**Harness-First Setup in Claude Code**
Before writing any feature prompt, build a context "harness" in Claude Code containing: (1) the product's design system (loaded via screenshots), (2) a context/strategy library (mission, prior features, user research), and (3) a prototyping skill. This front-loads the context so all subsequent prompts produce on-brand, informed output without re-explaining context each time.

**Live Product Audit**
During the problem-framing phase, open the actual product being discussed and walk through the real user flow to surface genuine friction points. In the video, this uncovered the broken Catch-up empty state, the bizarre notification coupling hiding the "job changes" feature, and the broken "Grow Your Network" CTA — all real, specific problems that ground the solution in reality rather than theory.

**Four-Element Prompt Framework**
A good prototyping prompt must include: (1) clear task steps ("generate five distinct UI concepts"), (2) context definition (user, goal, constraints), (3) explicit output format and requirements, and (4) stated non-goals or exclusions. Applies equally to any AI tool or model.

**Concept Evaluation Scoring**
When multiple design concepts are generated, evaluate each against three axes: (1) innovation/differentiation, (2) potential to drive the success metric (volume/reach), and (3) surface area / frequency of user encounter. Concepts that score well on all three advance; concepts that score well on only one dimension (e.g., visually innovative but low-reach) become lower priority or background features.

**Weak-Tie Reconnection Frame**
A product strategy frame specific to social/professional networks: the highest latent value lies in "weak ties" — first-degree connections you haven't interacted with recently — because they connect you to information and opportunities your close network can't. Features should surface these connections with genuine contextual reasons to reach out (shared events, career transitions, mutual problems) rather than generic notifications, to overcome the social awkwardness of cold outreach.
