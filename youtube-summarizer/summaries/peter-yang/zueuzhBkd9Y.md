# I Tested Gemini 4 vs Opus 5.5 vs GPT-6 Astra vs Muse Wonder (5 Use Cases)

Video ID: `zueuzhBkd9Y`

## Summary
The video is a head-to-head comparison of four frontier AI models — Anthropic's Opus 5.5, Google's Gemini 4, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, and Meta's Muse Wonder — across five practical use cases: 3D modeling/video, game building, animated short films, website design, and writing/research. The creator argues that no single model dominates all categories, and the right choice depends entirely on the task at hand. It is most relevant to AI power users, content creators, developers, and knowledge workers who want to understand where each model excels rather than looking for a single winner.

## Key insights
- **Opus 5.5 leads on creative/visual output**: It produced the most polished 3D render of Himiji Castle (cinematic quality, accurate architecture, traditional Japanese flute music), the best-looking Star Fox-style game (great camera angles, sound effects, wingman chatter, one-shotted), and the most technically impressive animated short. The creator called it "the most flashy" model overall.
- **Gemini 4 is the strongest runner-up and writing/research leader**: It came second in 3D and games, and it surprised the creator by winning the newsletter writing test because it was "the only draft that sounds like you." The Google team reportedly invested heavily in reducing Gemini's historically verbose writing style.
- **Gemini 4 excels at medical/deep research**: The creator used it for real personal research into his mother's health issues and found it surfaced information the other frontier models missed. Its verbosity — a historical weakness — is actually an asset for research-heavy tasks.
- **GPT-6 Astra won the website design test**: Its version was clean, minimal, had a clear CTA, and cleverly used ChatGPT's built-in image generation to add real photos and illustrations — something the other models didn't leverage. The creator noted OpenAI is not known for design sense, making this a surprise win.
- **Muse Wonder underperformed across the board on visual/generative tasks**: Its 3D castle looked like output from several model generations ago, its game was primitive, and its website was described as "reading a newspaper." Meta's chief AI officer acknowledged the model was not trained on 3D models.
- **Muse Wonder's best moment was the emotional short film**: Despite poor graphics, its story — an old man growing a flower to place on his late spouse's tombstone — was the most emotionally resonant of the four, catching the creator off guard.
- **Gemini 4 tends to over-build features**: In both the game and the website tests, Gemini packed in more features and content than needed (first-person view, barrel rolls, battle cruiser summon in the game; overwhelming copy on the website). The creator noted Gemini "tries the hardest" but sometimes overshoots.
- **Writing quality was evaluated using an Opus sub-agent as judge**: The sub-agent compared each model's newsletter output against the creator's final human-edited copy on three dimensions — personal tone, insightfulness, and detail match — with no prior context. Gemini ranked first, Opus second (most insightful but least personal), and Astra third (accurate but flat).
- **Gemini 4 is a significant upgrade over Gemini 3.8**: The creator explicitly noted the writing improvement is a major generational leap, attributing it to deliberate effort by the Google team to fix verbosity.
- **Astra's advantage in website design partly came from tool integration**: It natively leveraged ChatGPT image generation to populate the site with real visuals. The creator noted Gemini could theoretically do the same via "Nano Banana" but didn't, suggesting a prompt or configuration gap rather than a capability gap.

## Use cases
- **Developers and creative technologists** building 3D visualizations, animations, or cinematic renders with code (Blender scripting, etc.) → use Opus 5.5
- **Indie game developers** or prototypers wanting to one-shot a playable game → use Opus 5.5 for polish, Gemini 4 for feature breadth
- **Content creators and newsletter writers** who need a model that can match a personal, human-sounding voice → use Gemini 4
- **Researchers, analysts, or caregivers** doing deep medical, scientific, or domain research where comprehensiveness matters → use Gemini 4
- **Marketers or product teams** building landing pages or brand homepages from scratch → use GPT-6 Astra for clean, minimal design
- **Storytellers or filmmakers** wanting AI-generated emotional narrative content → Muse Wonder or Opus 5.5 depending on whether emotional resonance or visual quality matters more
- **Anyone evaluating which frontier model to use** for a specific workflow rather than picking a single "best" model overall

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Use-case-first model selection**: Rather than declaring a single winner, the creator applies a simple framework — test each model on the specific task type that matters to you, then select the best fit per task. The five task categories (3D/video, games, animated shorts, web design, writing/research) serve as a practical taxonomy for knowledge workers.
- **One-shot quality benchmark**: All models are given identical prompts with no follow-up or iteration. The output quality on the first attempt is the metric. This isolates raw model capability from prompt engineering skill and human-in-the-loop refinement.
- **AI-as-judge evaluation**: For the writing test, the creator used an Opus sub-agent (with no conversation context) to score all three drafts against his final human-edited copy on three rubric dimensions: personal tone, insightfulness, and detail accuracy. This is a repeatable pattern for evaluating writing quality without relying on purely subjective human taste.
- **Recurring benchmark task (Star Fox test)**: The creator uses a consistent prompt — build a Star Fox-style rail shooter — each time a new model releases, to create a longitudinal benchmark across model generations. This is a simple, memorable regression test for generative code/game capability.
- **Skill-based newsletter pipeline**: The creator has a reusable "skill" (a structured prompt workflow) that converts raw interview transcripts into formatted newsletter posts with intro and takeaways. Testing frontier models against this existing skill output is a real-world productivity benchmark rather than a synthetic task.
