## Summary
This video compares Google's Gemini 4 Neon against Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 across five practical use cases: 3D video generation, game creation, website redesign, writing, and advice/pushback resistance. The creator argues that Neon is the first Google model to genuinely reach Opus-level performance for everyday work, though Opus still leads in 3D, motion graphics, and design taste. The video is most relevant to AI power users, content creators, and product builders trying to decide which frontier model to use as their daily driver.

## Key insights
- **Benchmark scores don't tell the full story:** Neon beats Opus across most benchmarks except agentic coding tasks, but real-world use cases reveal more nuance — Opus still wins on creative and visual tasks.
- **Google's three-model lineup:** Carbon (deepest reasoning, like Fable), Neon (balanced frontier workhorse, like Opus), Hydrogen (fast/everyday, like Sonnet). The creator recommends Neon for 99% of Gemini use, reserving Carbon only for large codebase audits or major migrations.
- **3D video:** Opus produced a more cinematic Shibuya Crossing video (dramatic zoom, train in background, more pedestrians). Neon was decent but the intersection looked too small. Carbon was worst — no real intersection visible, no train.
- **Game generation (Godzilla/Shibuya):** Opus was more visually impressive with atmospheric intro, better destruction effects, and more abilities (Magma Breath) — but laggy even on an M5 MacBook. Neon ran smoother but had PS1-era graphics and plasma breath coming from Godzilla's neck. Carbon was broken (camera detached from Godzilla movement).
- **Website redesign:** Neon produced a cleaner design than Carbon (which over-stuffed the layout with text). Opus was most creative with bold motion graphics and an unconventional "clothes hanger" UI concept. Gemini models generally stuff too much text into landing page designs.
- **Writing (blind test):** Three essays on a software engineer reflecting on AI-replaced coding. Creator ranked Neon (Essay A) first, Carbon (Essay C) second, Opus (Essay B) third — noting Opus fell into "AI slop" patterns in the conclusion ("They are. I'm the part that moved."). All three were considered close in quality.
- **Writing style difference:** Neon gives concise, scannable feedback (3–4 bullet points with before/after examples). Carbon is verbose, burying the same information in harder-to-parse formats. Opus falls between the two.
- **Advice & pushback resistance:** All three models held their ground well under five pushback techniques (emotional appeals, contradicting data, social proof, fake facts). The only concession any model made was when presented with real data. Creator slightly preferred Opus's advice for being more nuanced and not demanding the interviews be cut entirely.
- **Overall ranking:** Creator ranks AI labs as Claude #1, Gemini #2, ChatGPT #3 as of recording — but notes the frontier shifts every few weeks.
- **Google AI Ultra plan ($100/month):** Includes Carbon, Neon, Hydrogen access + YouTube Premium Family Plan + 20 TB cloud storage — framed as strong value.

## Patterns & frameworks

**The "5 Use Case" Evaluation Framework**
Rather than trusting benchmarks, the creator tests every new frontier model against the same five tasks: 3D video, game creation, website redesign, writing, and pushback resistance. This creates a consistent, apples-to-apples comparison across model generations.

**The 99/1 Model Rule**
Pick one model within a provider's lineup as your default for 99% of work; reserve the most powerful/expensive model for the 1% of tasks that genuinely need it (e.g., auditing a massive codebase). Prevents over-spending and over-thinking model selection day-to-day.

**The Pushback Resistance Test**
Ask a model for advice, then challenge it five ways: emotional appeal, contradicting data, social proof, authority claim, and a fake fact. A good model holds its position on all except when given genuine new information. This tests whether the model is a yes-machine or a real advisor.

**Model-Agnostic Skill/Workflow System**
Store prompts, skills, and workflows in a version-controlled repo (e.g., private GitHub) so they can be used with any AI harness or model. The durable investment is the system, not loyalty to any one model — because the best model changes every few weeks.

**Blind Writing Comparison**
To remove bias when evaluating AI writing quality, generate essays from multiple models without knowing which is which, read and rank them, then reveal the source. Forces honest evaluation based on the actual output rather than brand preference.
