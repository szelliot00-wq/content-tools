# We Built an AI CFO to Help You Grow Your Net Worth | Anthony Pompliano

Video ID: `JXHStIulntQ`

## Summary
Anthony Pompliano (Pomp) joins Peter Yang to discuss his career trajectory from Facebook employee to investor, content creator, and now founder of Silvia — an AI CFO product for independent investors and high-net-worth individuals. The conversation covers Pomp's opportunistic career philosophy, hiring practices at small startups, and the strategic rationale behind building a specialized AI product in personal finance. The core argument is that specialized AI with proprietary data and domain-specific workflows will outcompete general-purpose models in verticals like wealth management. The video is most relevant to founders, product managers, investors, and high-net-worth individuals interested in AI-powered financial tools and startup building.

## Key Insights

- **Opportunistic career > 10-year plan:** Pomp never planned his career in advance. He identifies the biggest problem he has at any given moment and solves it — Facebook taught him scaling, investing taught him trend-spotting, and a personal spreadsheet pain point spawned Silvia.
- **Audience as currency:** Pomp learned from working on Zuckerberg's Facebook audience growth that audience would become a competitive moat. He built his own in 2016 specifically to gain deal flow access for investing.
- **Work trials over traditional interviews:** Silvia uses 2–4 week paid work trials instead of lengthy interview processes. Candidates get Slack access and real tasks; the company can assess communication, quality, and culture fit in the same time a traditional interview process would take, while also shipping actual features.
- **Hire for agency, not credentials:** Bringing in big-company hires with strong resumes consistently failed — they were trained for resource-rich, risk-managed environments incompatible with a fast-moving startup. Pomp now specifically avoids this profile.
- **Revenue-first hiring math:** When evaluating a hire, Pomp asks "can we grow revenue from $100 to $130 if we add this person?" rather than thinking about margin compression. Roles with no revenue impact are kept minimal.
- **Silvia started as a ChatGPT wrapper:** 18 months ago, it was a GPT model fed personal finance context. Over time they rebuilt the entire infrastructure, trained their own models, and built a custom harness with model routing, tool calling, and agents.
- **Specialized AI beats general AI in this vertical:** Silvia's "Silvia analyst" proprietary coding agent and their own trained models outperform frontier models on tax, mortgage, and credit card topics. They argue general models can't win here because they'll never route queries to competing models and can't match domain-specific workflow depth.
- **Average Silvia user has $2.5M connected:** This is not a budgeting app — it targets complexity. Wealthy users have philanthropy decisions, estate planning, entity structures, and tax optimization needs that generic fintech ignores.
- **25% conversion to paid; 20+ questions/week:** Among monthly actives, 25% are paying subscribers, and paying users ask 20+ questions per week — a strong signal of product-market fit.
- **People share more with AI than with human advisors:** Users ask questions they'd be embarrassed to ask a human expert, leading to richer context, better answers, and a compounding improvement loop.
- **Privacy as a product feature:** Bank-level encryption, SOC 2, PII anonymization, zero data retention with LLM providers, and optional manual asset entry (no account linking required) address the trust barrier.
- **Memory as a generational tool:** Users can annotate assets with intent ("here's why I bought this"), creating a financial diary or instruction manual for heirs — enabled by Silvia's custom-built memory and file system.
- **Defined benefit plan example:** A user with persistently high income and a solo-owner entity was told by Silvia to set up a defined benefit plan, saving hundreds of thousands annually in taxes — only possible because Silvia had full financial context.
- **Crypto at ~10% allocation:** Silvia data across tens of thousands of users shows ~10% allocation to Bitcoin/crypto, higher than traditionalist advice (0–3%) but far below maximalist views. Pomp frames Bitcoin as an inflation hedge on government money printing.
- **Culture fit > good/bad culture:** There's no universally good or bad culture — only employee-company fit. The same environment that energizes one person will repel another.

## Patterns & Frameworks

**Opportunistic career navigation**
Rather than working backwards from a 10-year goal, identify the biggest unsolved problem in front of you right now and go solve it. Each job or venture becomes a platform to learn the specific skill needed for the next move (scaling at Facebook, deal-flow at a VC fund, domain expertise at Silvia).

**Work trial hiring process**
2–4 week paid trials where candidates get full system access and a real task with minimal direction. Evaluates productivity, quality, communication, feedback receptiveness, and culture fit simultaneously — in the same calendar time as a traditional interview loop, while also producing shipped work.

**Revenue-impact hiring calculus**
Frame every hire as: "If I'm currently at $100 revenue, can adding this person get me to $130?" Minimizes non-revenue roles; keeps headcount lean and output-focused.

**Specialized AI stack (Silvia's intelligence ownership model)**
Four-layer approach: (1) custom harness with model router and tool-calling agents, (2) proprietary coding agent trained on internal tasks and architecture, (3) domain-fine-tuned models outperforming frontier models on specific topics, (4) proprietary dataset from real user interactions enabling RLHF with domain-specific rubrics. The strategic bet: general intelligence for general tasks, specialized intelligence for high-complexity vertical use cases.

**Context-depth flywheel**
The more financial context a user provides, the more accurate and personalized Silvia's answers become. Users are more candid with AI than human advisors, which feeds better context into the model, which produces better answers, which increases engagement — a self-reinforcing loop.

**Employee-company culture fit model**
Culture is not intrinsically good or bad — it must match the individual. The test: when told "this role has no 20-person team under you," does the candidate's face light up or fall? The reaction itself is the filter.

**Problem-solver identity framework (parenting/talent)**
Instill "we solve problems" as a core identity rather than prescribing a specific career or life path. Applied both in how Pomp raises his kids and how he evaluates talent — agency and problem-orientation matter more than credentials or prior titles.
