# Why OpenAI is calling for a ‘cyber defense surge.’ Plus: Find Evil! winners and TeamPCP losers

Video ID: `-0p68wKEitE`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's Security Intelligence podcast covers three main topics: OpenAI's open letter calling for a global surge in AI-powered cyber defense (signed by 100+ organizations including IBM), the results of the SANS Institute's "Find Evil" hackathon to develop autonomous AI security agents, and the unmasking and arrest of alleged members of hacking group Team PCP, who were caught through basic credential reuse. Panelists from IBM X-Force discuss practical implications for defenders across all three stories.

## Key insights
- **Collective defense over individual suffering:** The core message of the OpenAI letter is that organizations shouldn't each learn from being attacked — sharing remediation strategies and patches (not just threat intel) is the next evolution in collaborative security.
- **Defenders are asymmetrically constrained:** AI guardrails restrict legitimate defenders more than attackers, who simply remove restrictions. This is pushing some security researchers toward open-weight models with fewer limitations.
- **AI agents excel at investigation, not response:** The Find Evil hackathon winners were strong at forensic analysis and timeline reconstruction, but panelists agreed human-in-the-loop remains essential for high-consequence actions like shutting down servers or revoking credentials.
- **Self-questioning AI is a meaningful breakthrough:** Standout hackathon entries pushed back on their own findings and even their own briefings — a capability that matters more than raw speed in incident response.
- **Hallucination remains a blocker for full autonomy:** Even self-critical agents can hallucinate about their own self-questioning, so human review of AI-generated investigation outputs is still required.
- **Team PCP was undone by ego and password reuse:** Investigators traced the group by matching a handle ("dead Cat X3") across platforms including a Steam gaming account, then confirming reused passwords — the same hygiene failures that catch ordinary users.
- **Disclosing how attackers are caught is a double-edged sword:** Publishing investigative methods (as Flare did) deters other groups and shows capability, but also tips off remaining threat actors to clean up their operational security.
- **Critical infrastructure will lag behind:** Banks and tech firms will adopt AI defenses fastest; hospitals, municipalities, and water systems risk being left behind in any "surge" — a gap the collective effort must deliberately address.
