# How AI Is Changing Code Reviews & Software Development

Video ID: `c57vAe-mMLo`

## Summary
The video traces the evolution of code review practices from Michael Fagan's structured team inspections in the early days of software development through paired programming, pull requests, and CI/CD automation. It argues that AI represents a fundamental shift in this trajectory — moving focus away from *how* code is implemented toward *whether* the right thing was built. The central thesis is that AI handles broad technical analysis while humans provide context, judgment, and business reasoning.

## Key insights
- **Four eras of code review:** Fagan inspections (structured team syntax review) → Agile paired programming (on-the-fly review) → Pull request era (consensus/approval-based review) → CI/CD automation era (system-assisted compliance and quality checks).
- **AI marks a fifth era — outcome reviews:** The shift is from validating *implementation correctness* to validating *intent, requirements, and business impact*.
- **Division of labor between AI and humans:** AI takes on broad analysis — writing code and docs, reviewing diffs, enforcing quality/compliance checks — while humans focus on setting context, applying judgment, and evaluating trade-offs against business goals.
- **Prompt-driven iteration replaces line-by-line review:** Developers now iterate through prompts rather than inspecting code directly, comparing AI-generated output against desired outcomes.
- **Reviews become evidence-based:** Rather than checking syntax or seeking consensus, modern reviews ask "did we build what we intended?" using automated tests, runtime data, and business requirement traceability as evidence.
- **The future of software development is human + AI collaboration:** Humans do what humans do best (strategic thinking, goal-setting, trade-off decisions); AI does what it does best (managing complexity across large, deeply integrated systems).
