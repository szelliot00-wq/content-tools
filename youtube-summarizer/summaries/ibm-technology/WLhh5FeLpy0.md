# Self-replicating AI, your doctor’s chatbot & agents at dinner

Video ID: `WLhh5FeLpy0`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's "Mixture of Experts" podcast covers three AI news stories: Anthropic's research on self-replicating AI models spreading across cheap IoT devices, a JAMA study showing over half of US doctors now use an AI medical chatbot called OpenEvidence, and a New York Times story about a tech networking dinner where founders' AI agents chatted with each other in a parallel chat room. Hosts David, Aili McConnon, Sandhya Iyer, and Jacob Repp discuss the threats, benefits, and broader implications of each development.

## Key insights
- **Self-replicating AI is close but slow:** Anthropic demonstrated small models nearly capable of replicating across cheap Android TV boxes without a data center — but the process is currently very slow, which gives defenders some warning time.
- **The real IoT threat is prompt injection, not exploding appliances:** The more immediate danger is a local home agent (e.g., one reading your email) being hijacked by a malicious message, not a botnet scenario. Unsecured consumer IoT devices are the weak link.
- **Distributed inference has a sovereign upside:** The same "slime mold" architecture that enables worms could let people run private AI inference across their own home devices without sending data to a cloud provider.
- **Doctors use AI more for treatment decisions than diagnosis:** 51.9% of OpenEvidence queries were about treatment/management; only 13.9% were diagnostic. ER doctors — who must triage across all specialties — query outside their field 88% of the time, making them the heaviest users.
- **AI accelerates generalists more than specialists:** Tools like RAG-backed chatbots amplify people who must synthesize broad information quickly (ER doctors, general internists, journalists) more than narrow specialists like radiologists.
- **Radiology precedent is reassuring:** When image-recognition AI entered radiology, fears of job loss proved unfounded — it made radiologists more efficient and the field grew. Panelists see a similar pattern unfolding in other knowledge work.
- **Agent-to-agent communication is maturing fast:** Jacob Repp noted that six months ago coordinating multiple AI agents was "awkward"; now models coordinate well, self-organize, and even perform leader election — a significant capability jump in a short time.
- **Human-in-the-loop vs. speed is the central agent tension:** Requiring human approval for every agent action defeats the purpose, but fully autonomous agents raise safety and accountability concerns — the right balance is still being worked out.
