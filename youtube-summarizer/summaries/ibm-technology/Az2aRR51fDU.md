# Jensen Huang exits NVIDIA & does Claude Fable 5.2 count as AGI?

Video ID: `Az2aRR51fDU`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's "Mixture of Experts" covers two major AI news stories: the resignation of NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang and Anthropic's release of Claude Fable 5.2, with a panel debating whether the new model constitutes AGI. The episode also features an interview with Sean Falconer of Confluent, who introduces the "Streamhouse" data architecture designed to give AI agents access to real-time business state. Throughout, the panel explores broader themes of creativity, human identity, and what gets lost or gained as AI becomes more capable.

## Key insights
- **Jensen Huang's departure is historically significant.** NVIDIA is unusually founder-dependent — Huang's long tenure enabled long-term bets like CUDA that now define the company's moat. His exit creates a culture shock with no clear parallel in tech.
- **NVIDIA's moat is deeper than GPUs.** CUDA, high-bandwidth networking, DPUs, and chips like Grace Hopper (with 900 GB/s interconnect) represent a hardware ecosystem that competitors' "rev one" products are unlikely to displace quickly.
- **Huang's geopolitical stance mattered.** He was a consistent advocate for free trade with China on chips — his departure could shift NVIDIA's political posture in ways that ripple through global AI supply chains.
- **Fable 5.2 impresses practitioners but doesn't meet a stable AGI bar.** The panel agrees the goalposts for AGI keep moving — what would have seemed like science fiction three years ago is now baseline. Real-world deployment still requires substantial scaffolding and harness work beyond the model itself.
- **The AGI debate is partly a marketing debate.** New model releases are product launches, and the awe they generate is partly engineered. Practitioners note that a model alone is not enough to make a working AI system.
- **AI is redefining creative professions, including software engineering.** Panelists describe mourning the loss of coding as a personal creative practice while also recognizing new creative unlocks — building sophisticated software previously out of reach for most people.
- **Human authorship still matters to audiences, even when AI output is emotionally affecting.** The panel argues that art involves an irreducible human-to-human connection through a medium; AI output, however technically impressive, is experienced as a curio rather than genuine expression.
- **Streamhouse architecture addresses a core AI-agent problem: stale data.** Centralized data warehouses and lakehouses always lag behind real-time business state. Streamhouse uses change as the fundamental primitive so agents reason on current truth, not delayed snapshots.
- **The water metaphor evolves:** streams feed lakes — streams carry time-sensitive, high-value data; lakes store retrospective, analytical data. Both matter, but AI agents operating autonomously need the stream.
