# OpenAI talks GPT-6 Astra and Millenium Prize, researchers create WeWorm exploit & IBM’s US Open app

Video ID: `XPReiOKCzFI`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's "Mixture of Experts" podcast covers three main topics: OpenAI's new Astra model and its claimed solution to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem, IBM's AI-powered fan experience at the US Open tennis tournament, and a security story about a WeChat worm exploit discovered using AI. The panel — composed of IBM AI engineers and a staff writer — discusses the real-world implications of frontier AI capabilities, balancing excitement about scientific breakthroughs with grounded skepticism about computational costs and enterprise relevance.

## Key insights
- **Astra's real-world enterprise value is questioned**: Despite impressive demos (2D-to-3D rendering, computer control), panelists noted that current enterprise use cases don't yet require a model of that magnitude — speed improvements exist, but the cost-benefit is unclear.
- **No consensus definition of AGI exists**: The panel cautioned against declaring Astra as AGI, noting the community lacks a converged definition, and AGI might simply look like a very capable digital worker when it arrives.
- **Navier-Stokes proof required ~$15M in compute to win a $1M prize**: OpenAI deployed 10,000 AI agents over 88 hours to solve the 90-year-old fluid dynamics problem, with the proof later verified in 17 hours using the Lean formal proof system.
- **Human judgment was essential to the Navier-Stokes breakthrough**: Humans identified which problem to focus on and made key strategic decisions throughout — the result is framed as a collaborative human-AI achievement, not purely autonomous AI.
- **AI lowers the barrier to finding exploits, not just the sophistication**: The WeChat zero-click worm (discovered by startup Calypso) illustrates that AI makes vulnerability discovery more *accessible*, not necessarily more powerful — a meaningful distinction for the threat landscape.
- **All software has vulnerabilities; AI just surfaces them faster**: Olivia argued that imperfect software is an inherent human condition, and AI tools are now exploring "unconventional attack surfaces" with less specific prompting than was previously required.
- **Compute cost acts as a real-world constraint on AI-driven attacks**: Creating worms or solving Millennium problems both require massive resources, so the risk is proportional to who controls those resources and what their priorities are.
- **IBM's US Open platform tracks biomechanics at scale**: The system processes 1.2 billion data points across 254 matches, tracking 21 body points at 50 fps to analyze serve quality — a first-of-its-kind live sports AI application.
- **Optimism about AI in medicine**: Panelists expressed genuine excitement that the same multi-agent approaches used on Navier-Stokes could accelerate breakthroughs in cancer research and other major global challenges.
