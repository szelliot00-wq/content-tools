# An Ancient Guide to Cybersecurity: 8 Lessons from The Art of War

Video ID: `kjoQPn--F7A`

## Summary
The video maps eight principles from Sun Tzu's *The Art of War* onto modern cybersecurity practice, arguing that timeless military strategy translates directly to network defense. The presenter covers topics ranging from threat intelligence and prevention-first thinking to deception tactics, speed of response, and resource conservation. The throughline is that defenders who think strategically — rather than reactively — are better positioned against adaptive adversaries.

## Key insights
- **Know yourself and your enemy (threat intelligence):** Map your own attack surface before attackers do, and study adversary TTPs (tools, tactics, techniques, and procedures) to fight smarter.
- **Win before you fight (prevention over response):** Patching, hardening, MFA, passkeys, and zero-trust architecture stop attacks before they land — if you're doing incident response, you've already lost round one.
- **Deception as defense:** Honeypots, honey tokens (e.g., fake AWS keys in config files), and canary files (e.g., `passwords.xls`) lure attackers into revealing themselves while wasting their time on fake targets.
- **Speed and adaptability:** Mean time to identify and contain a breach averages nearly two-thirds of a year; AI-powered detection and response must compress that window, since attackers increasingly operate at machine speed.
- **Pick your battles (risk-based prioritization):** You can't protect everything equally — identify your "crown jewels," perform risk analysis, and allocate defenses accordingly rather than treating all assets the same.
- **Control the terrain (visibility and segmentation):** Extensive monitoring, network segmentation, and DMZs force attackers through choke points where defenders have full visibility; AI helps filter signal from noise at scale.
- **Leadership is the fortress:** Security culture flows top-down; a CISO must manage both upward (executive buy-in, funding) and downward (team morale and alignment). Untrained leadership may be a bigger vulnerability than unpatched software.
- **Preserve resources (asymmetry awareness):** Defenders have limited budgets and must be right every time; attackers only need to succeed once. Automation and AI offload routine work so humans can focus on higher-order threats.
