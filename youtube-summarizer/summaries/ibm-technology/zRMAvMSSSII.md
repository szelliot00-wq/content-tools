# Human in the Loop Is Not a Governance Strategy for Agentic AI

Video ID: `zRMAvMSSSII`

## Summary
This video challenges the common assumption that having "a human in the loop" constitutes real AI governance. The speaker argues that a human reviewing AI output is not the same as a governed system, and maps the five components of human trust (credibility, reliability, dependability, self-orientation, integrity) to five technical AI properties. They conclude by defining what meaningful human oversight actually requires, and call out the exclusion of social science expertise from AI design.

## Key insights
- "A human reviewed it" is not the same as "the system is governed" — review without authority, visibility, or feedback loops is theater, not governance.
- Trust in human judgment maps directly to AI system properties: credibility → transparency, reliability → explainability, dependability → observability, self-orientation → algorithm optimization, integrity → adversarial robustness.
- Observability is especially critical for agentic AI, where conditions change dynamically and the system must be monitored for ongoing correctness — not just at a single output checkpoint.
- Human reviewers bring something models cannot replicate: the ability to read circumstances, not just data, and to recognize when a case file tells one story and a person's life tells another.
- Social workers, ethicists, historians, and counselors have long been excluded from technology decisions on the grounds that their expertise isn't "technical" — yet they are precisely the people equipped to reason about systems that affect millions of lives.
- Real human-in-the-loop governance requires four things: visibility into AI reasoning, authority to stop the process, the right measurements, and feedback loops — anything less is "counting warm bodies and calling it governance."
