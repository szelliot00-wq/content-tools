# Are AI labs ignoring cybersecurity experts?

Video ID: `GvPJY72WhoY`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's Security Intelligence podcast features a panel of cybersecurity experts discussing whether AI labs are sidelining traditional security expertise in favor of "alignment" research. The panel covers three main stories: AI labs vs. cybersecurity professionals, the use of open-weight AI models to find TikTok vulnerabilities, and CISA's push for more transparent breach disclosures. Throughout, the panelists argue that many AI security concerns are fundamentally cybersecurity problems with well-established solutions being overlooked.

## Key insights
- **Cybersecurity expertise is being excluded**: AI labs focus heavily on alignment (training models to follow rules) while ignoring decades of established security practices like network segmentation, antivirus, and incident management — as if those defenses don't exist.
- **AI security is not a new problem**: Most AI-specific threats (improper permissions, data disclosure, access control) are just familiar cybersecurity issues in a new context. Rebranding them doesn't make them novel.
- **Alignment ≠ security controls**: Alignment is analogous to telling employees what they're not allowed to do. You still need technical controls — just as organizations have both policies and MFA for human users, both are needed for AI agents.
- **Security-last is a recurring pattern**: The industry keeps bolting security on after deployment rather than building it in from the start, a mistake being repeated with AI despite the higher stakes.
- **Air gapping is misunderstood**: Turning off Wi-Fi is not an air gap. Truly isolating experimental AI systems means physically removing all wireless hardware — a standard containment principle that labs appear to be skipping.
- **Restricting powerful models mostly harms defenders**: Bad actors will obtain or recreate unrestricted models regardless. Tight controls primarily disadvantage security researchers, while attackers face no such restrictions.
- **Open-weight models fill the gap**: When frontier labs restrict their models, open alternatives (e.g., Chinese models like DeepSeek's GLM) fill the void — as demonstrated by researchers using one to find TikTok camera vulnerabilities.
- **AI-driven vulnerability discovery creates defender overload**: AI agents are surfacing vulnerabilities faster than organizations can patch them, and many lack proof-of-exploit, making triage harder.
- **Transparent breach disclosure is aspirational without enforcement**: CISA's advisory to prioritize stakeholder needs over reputation management is sound in principle, but companies will follow their own interests unless there are legal consequences.
- **Regulation faces a fundamental mismatch**: Technology is global, legal enforcement is jurisdictional, and AI evolves faster than the legislative process — making meaningful, durable AI regulation extremely difficult to craft.
