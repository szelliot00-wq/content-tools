# Stripe buys OpenRouter, Ramp’s AI Index & IBM’s OpenAI deal

Video ID: `8LNH7TxvD14`

## Summary
This episode of *Mixture of Experts* covers three major AI business stories: IBM's new partnership with OpenAI (alongside its existing Anthropic deal), Stripe's $7B acquisition of OpenRouter, and Ramp's 2026 AI Index data revealing enterprise spending patterns. The panel — IBM researchers and executives — argues that value in AI is shifting away from the models themselves toward orchestration, routing, governance, and system integration. A brief closing segment touches on AI-generated legislative "slop" flooding the US House of Representatives.

## Key insights
- **IBM is positioning as a vendor-neutral enterprise AI orchestrator**, partnering with both Anthropic (for software engineering) and OpenAI (for consulting), while retaining its own Granite models for cheaper, simpler tasks — avoiding the expensive race to build frontier models.
- **There is no single "best" model** — the right model depends on cost, latency, geographic availability, rate limits, and task type, which is exactly what makes routing and integration layers valuable.
- **Stripe bought OpenRouter for the tollbooth, not the technology** — token prices have dropped 90%+ but volume is exploding, so sitting between all model providers and collecting a margin on every request is a more durable business than building models.
- **OpenRouter's valuation jumped 5x in 82 days** (from $1.3B to $7B), reflecting how rapidly the market is repricing infrastructure-layer AI companies.
- **Routing will likely commoditize** — there are already 100+ open-source and commercial AI gateways (LiteLLM, Portkey, Helicone, Cloudflare, etc.), so the moat is narrow and the window may be short.
- **Enterprise AI is entering a FinOps era** — Ramp data across 70,000 businesses shows token spend up 13x in a year, but CFOs are now demanding measurable ROI rather than approving open-ended experimentation budgets.
- **AI cost distribution is extremely uneven**: top 1% of companies spend ~$7,400/employee/month on AI; the median is just $12 — suggesting most organizations are barely scratching the surface while a few are overexposed.
- **Runaway agent costs are a hidden problem** — it's not individual ChatGPT users burning tokens; it's undocumented agent fleets running autonomously at scale with no governance or budget controls.
- **Engineering teams are the one department where AI spend keeps growing without pushback** — tools like Cursor and Claude Code have crossed from novelty to essential infrastructure; other departments haven't cleared that bar yet.
- **AI-generated legislation is already appearing in the US House**, mirroring the explosion of AI-written papers, emails, and Slack messages — the panel argues the answer is governance and clear guidelines, not prohibition.
