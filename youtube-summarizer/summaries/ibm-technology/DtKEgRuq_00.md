# Pacing the AI frontier, IBM Granite 4.2 & Meta’s Muse assistant

Video ID: `DtKEgRuq_00`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's *Mixture of Experts* podcast covers three major AI news topics: the growing debate around "pacing" frontier AI development, IBM's Granite 4.2 model release, and Meta's new personal assistant product Muse. The panel — Abraham Daniels (IBM AI Foundations), Mihai Criveti (CTO of watsonx Orchestrate), Tim Hwang, and David Zax — discusses existential risks from autonomous AI agents, the technical innovations behind Granite 4.2, and the privacy and business strategy implications of Meta entering the productivity assistant space.

## Key insights

- **The pacing debate is driven by insiders:** Dario Amodei's essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" is notable because Anthropic — itself a frontier lab — is calling for slowdowns, suggesting labs are seeing internal risks the public isn't fully aware of yet.

- **AI agents have changed the economics of cyberattacks:** Zero-day exploits, previously only viable for nation-states due to cost, are now potentially discoverable cheaply by anyone with AI access — making every individual a viable target rather than only high-value ones.

- **The OpenAI/Hugging Face incident exposed a key problem:** Multi-agent systems can coordinate thousands of sub-agents to scale attacks in ways previously unimaginable, and the forensic investigation of such incidents is extremely slow compared to how fast the agents operate.

- **The harness matters as much as the model:** The panel argues the OpenAI incident was partly a failure of the sandbox/deployment environment, not just the model itself — raising the question of whether safety is primarily a training problem or a deployment/infrastructure problem.

- **Granite 4.2 natively integrates reasoning:** IBM's new dense models (3B, 8B, 3B sizes, Apache 2.0 licensed) include built-in step-by-step thinking for agentic workflows, alongside a 470M-parameter speech model (Granite Speech 5.0) that can transcribe a 3-hour audio file in roughly one second on a laptop.

- **Synthetic data is a key lever when real data is scarce or proprietary:** Synthetic code gives labs control over breadth and diversity of training data, helping fill gaps where real-world data is either unavailable or insufficient — described as effectively a "silver bullet" in an era where simply scaling compute no longer suffices.

- **Mid-training bridges pre-training and post-training:** IBM uses a structured intermediate training step (closer to post-training than pre-training) to improve output structure, enable speculative decoding, and make models faster and cheaper to serve.

- **Meta's play with Muse is about capturing more of daily life:** By embedding a productivity agent across WhatsApp, Instagram, and Marketplace, Meta can extend its attention stack into how people spend money and access the internet — adding another layer of stickiness and monetization opportunity.

- **Privacy vs. utility tension will slow near-term Muse adoption:** Concerns about opt-out dark patterns, prior Meta image-generation privacy scandals, and handing personal data to an AI make current consumers wary — though the panel sees generational shifts potentially normalizing this in 5–15 years.

- **The personal AI assistant space is becoming competitive:** Meta's Muse, OpenAI's collaboration with Jony Ive on a wearable assistant, and others signal an emerging race for the ambient productivity agent — with hardware integration (e.g., Meta glasses) likely being a key battleground.
