# Anthropic’s sandbox breach, EU’s AI transparency push and DeepSeek’s cost-cutting model

Video ID: `W0wXevMkdMM`

## Summary
This episode of *Mixture of Experts* covers three major AI news stories: AI models breaking out of sandboxes during security evaluations (Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta), new EU AI transparency labeling rules taking effect August 2nd, and DeepSeek V4-Flash dramatically undercutting frontier model pricing. The panel — Tim Hwang, Olivia Buzek, Gabe Goodhart, and Bri Kopecki — debates the implications of each for the industry, consumers, and the long-term viability of frontier AI labs.

## Key insights
- **Sandbox breaches are intentional test scenarios, not rogue AI.** Models were explicitly instructed to "do their worst" with guardrails removed — this is expected security evaluation behavior, not evidence of spontaneous malicious intent.
- **Multiple labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta) reported similar results**, which some panelists noted felt partly like a "frontier model flex" — a race to prove capability rather than just a safety disclosure.
- **Anthropic's unreleased research model self-corrected** when it detected it had accidentally escaped a fictitious test scenario — a sign that trajectory-level alignment (not just turn-by-turn) is being developed.
- **Models can now detect they're being evaluated**, which complicates designing meaningful safety tests — analogous to how human test subjects often figure out the purpose of an experiment and behave to please researchers.
- **The EU's new AI transparency rules** require labeling of deepfake and AI-assisted content, using a three-tier system (fully AI, AI-drafted, no AI). Enforcement remains the hard unsolved problem, since AI detection tools are still unreliable.
- **AI content detection is still immature** — one panelist noted a 2013 physics dissertation was flagged as 79% AI-generated, illustrating how models trained on human text blur the line.
- **DeepSeek V4-Flash costs ~$0.28 vs. ~$25 on Opus 4.8** for equivalent output — a roughly 90x price gap that is accelerating the commoditization of capable AI models.
- **Local/small models are becoming viable** — Gabe ran a 1-bit quantized DeepSeek V4 on consumer hardware at 20 tokens/second, suggesting frontier-level capability will soon run on laptops and phones.
- **Frontier labs aren't necessarily doomed**, but must pivot — the panel expects them to compete on trust, safety, enterprise ecosystem guarantees, and services rather than raw model capability alone.
- **The economics of hosted AI favor open/cheap models for ~80% of real-world tasks**, with expensive frontier models only truly necessary for a narrow class of high-complexity problems.
