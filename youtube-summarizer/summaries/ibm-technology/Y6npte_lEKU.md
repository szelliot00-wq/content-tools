# Anthropic goes public, ChatGPT gets a wallet & AI designs new drugs

Video ID: `Y6npte_lEKU`

## Summary
This episode of IBM's "Mixture of Experts" podcast covers three major AI news stories from mid-November 2025: Anthropic's IPO (dubbed "Grandthropic" alongside GTA VI's release), OpenAI's launch of ChatGPT Wallet for agentic commerce, and Isomorphic Labs' $5B Series C funding for AI-driven drug discovery. IBM experts Kush Varshney, Sridhar Rajagopalan, and Alexis Zamkow discuss the business, technical, and social implications of each.

## Key insights
- **Anthropic's IPO signals massive compute demand**: Amazon holds ~14% and Google ~13% of Anthropic post-IPO, primarily to supply cloud compute. Anthropic plans to diversify across hardware vendors (NVIDIA, AWS, Google chips), because capital now exceeds chip supply and AI is well-suited to optimizing across heterogeneous hardware.
- **Hardware diversification is now essential**: The abundance of investment capital in AI outstrips the supply of any single chip vendor, pushing companies toward multi-vendor strategies — a shift enabled by AI's ability to optimize across different hardware-software interfaces at scale.
- **Agentic commerce is nascent but accelerating**: Meta's Muse personal agent launched in October with ~11M downloads; OpenAI followed with ChatGPT Wallet. One-time virtual card numbers (just-in-time credentials) address the top consumer concern — fraud — by borrowing a security pattern already proven in enterprise IT/SRE operations.
- **AI drug discovery compresses the candidate search space**: The core bottleneck AI solves is pruning from quadrillions of possible molecules to a high-confidence shortlist *before* wet-lab work, dramatically improving the odds that candidates survive expensive clinical trials — meaning even the traditionally slow human-trial phase may effectively accelerate.
- **Toxicity prediction is as important as efficacy**: Many compounds kill cancer cells but are also toxic to the patient. Models like IBM's MoLFormer use controlled generation to filter for drug-like properties (solubility, low toxicity) up front, not just biological activity.
- **Biological simulation complements protein-shape prediction**: Beyond AlphaFold-style structure prediction, AI models learn from large datasets of molecular properties to predict real-world drug behavior without needing full physics-based chemical simulation.
- **AI history is rooted in games and simulated worlds**: From IBM's checkers-based coinage of "machine learning" to Deep Blue to modern world-model research, games have consistently been AI's proving ground — and GPU hardware built for gaming underpins today's AI infrastructure.
