# How Can Junior PMs Build Product Sense in the AI Era

Video ID: `yGe-SowXBvM`

## Summary
This short video features a product management coach addressing how junior (and all-level) PMs can build product sense in an era where AI handles much of the repetitive work — analysis, prototyping, spec writing — that traditionally taught those skills. The core argument is simple but counterintuitive: the answer is not to avoid AI tools, but to use them while paying intense, critical attention to their outputs. The speaker frames attention and critical evaluation as the rare, differentiating skill in the AI era. The video is most relevant to junior and mid-level PMs, though the speaker explicitly extends the advice to seniors and founders as well.

## Key insights
- **Use AI tools fully** — the speaker explicitly rejects the idea that PMs should avoid AI to "build skills the hard way." Use the tools, use them as much as makes sense.
- **Attention is the real skill** — the differentiating behavior is not what tool you use, but whether you carefully read, analyze, and decompose what the AI produces.
- **Most people are not paying attention** — the speaker observes that even high-caliber professionals (top product people and founders the speaker coaches) are not paying sufficient attention to AI outputs.
- **AI packages good and bad insights in equally polished language** — a critical point: AI outputs look uniformly well-written, which makes it harder to distinguish strong insights from weak ones without careful scrutiny.
- **This applies to all AI output formats** — chat answers, documents, prototypes, and any other AI-generated artifact all require the same critical evaluation.
- **Attention will differentiate you from 90%+ of peers** — the speaker frames this not as a basic tip but as a genuine competitive edge, precisely because so few people actually do it despite its apparent simplicity.
- **Think for yourself** — beyond just reading carefully, the speaker emphasizes active critical thinking: decomposing outputs, evaluating quality at the claim level, not accepting the whole package uncritically.

## Use cases
- A junior PM using AI to draft a PRD or spec — reading it critically to identify where the AI made sound recommendations vs. where it hallucinated or oversimplified.
- A mid-level PM reviewing an AI-generated competitive analysis — verifying claims rather than forwarding the output directly to stakeholders.
- A senior PM or founder using AI-assisted prototyping — evaluating the prototype's UX assumptions, not just whether it "looks good."
- Any PM in a performance review or job search trying to stand out — demonstrating genuine critical thinking about AI outputs as a differentiator.
- Coaching or mentorship contexts where PMs are being evaluated on judgment, not just output volume.

## Patterns & frameworks
- **Attention-as-skill framework**: The core mental model is reframing "attention" — careful reading, decomposition, and evaluation of AI outputs — as the primary skill to develop, rather than any specific PM technique. It works by treating every AI output as a draft requiring editorial judgment: What is good here? What is bad? Why?
- **Polish ≠ quality heuristic**: A named observation that AI wraps both strong and weak insights in fluent, confident language. The practical pattern is to evaluate the substance of each claim independently, ignoring the surface quality of the prose.
- **Use-and-scrutinize loop**: An implicit workflow pattern — generate with AI, then critically review before acting or sharing — as the repeatable process for building product sense through AI-assisted work rather than despite it.
