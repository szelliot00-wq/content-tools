# The LMS Framework

Video ID: `fuQcYT2cIuA`

## Summary
In this video, a former Google PM shares how he overcame early-career self-doubt and mediocre skills to become a top 50 product manager in Silicon Valley. He introduces the LMS Framework — a structured approach to career growth that prioritizes eliminating weaknesses, reaching median competence in weak areas, and converting natural strengths into superpowers. The talk draws heavily from his personal experience joining Google in 2008 while simultaneously becoming a new parent, using that period of pressure as the catalyst for deliberate skill development. It is most relevant to individual contributor PMs who want a clear, actionable model for accelerating their career trajectory.

## Key insights
- He joined Google in 2008 as a 2–3 year PM and felt far behind peers intellectually and skill-wise, yet his managers were "delighted" with his performance — illustrating that self-assessment and external perception can diverge sharply.
- He set a goal to become a top 50 PM in Silicon Valley at a time when he estimated he wasn't even in the top 1,000–2,000 — the goal was intentionally outsized as a forcing function for growth.
- He frames any good framework as "packaged intuition" — he didn't design the LMS approach analytically; he did it instinctively, then reverse-engineered it into a model.
- The three core IC PM skills he identifies are: **execution sense**, **analytical sense**, and **product sense**.
- At Google circa 2008, he rated himself: strong in product sense (natural gift, not earned), weak in analytical sense, and very weak in execution sense relative to peers.
- He learned through three modes: reading/self-study (books, side projects), doing (taking on projects that forced use of weak skills), and shadowing (sitting in on meetings run by strong PMs).
- The shadowing tactic was manager-facilitated — his manager sent a message to strong PMs on his behalf, and they agreed to let him observe team meetings without disruption. Two or three sessions were enough to fundamentally change his understanding of what good meeting facilitation looks like.
- He set the goal around 2009 and felt he had achieved it by roughly 2012–2014 — a 3–5 year window.
- Signals he used to validate reaching "top 50" status: exceptionally strong performance ratings, rapid promotions, unsolicited stock grants from Google, being identified as "top talent" in internal talent reviews (3×3 grid), HR staff outside his org recognizing his name, and consistently receiving job offers when interviewing externally at startups and mid-size companies.

## Use cases
- Early-to-mid career PMs who feel outmatched by peers and need a structured way to prioritize skill development.
- PMs joining highly competitive companies (FAANG-level) where the skill bar is unusually high.
- PMs who are naturally strong in one area (e.g., product sense or technical skills) but struggling in others (e.g., data analysis or stakeholder execution).
- Anyone trying to set a meaningful long-term career goal and reverse-engineer the skills needed to get there.
- PMs who want to use shadowing as a low-cost, high-signal learning tool — especially for soft skills like running effective meetings.
- Managers looking to coach junior PMs on how to build a deliberate development plan.

## Patterns & frameworks

**The LMS Framework**
A three-step model for PM career development, applied in sequence by priority:
1. **L — Eliminate Liabilities**: Identify skills where you are significantly below the median. Bring those up to at least median level so they stop holding you back. For him, this was execution sense and analytical sense.
2. **M — Reach the Median**: Specifically target below-median skills and close the gap through deliberate practice — doing projects that exercise those skills, shadowing strong practitioners, and self-study.
3. **S — Turn Strengths into Superpowers**: Once liabilities are neutralized, invest in making your natural strengths exceptional rather than merely good. For him, this was converting his natural product sense into a differentiating superpower.

The key insight is that PM is a generalist role — unlike engineering or design, you cannot succeed by doubling down only on strengths while ignoring weaknesses. Weaknesses will cap your ceiling.

**Three Modes of Deliberate Learning**
A supporting pattern for *how* to develop skills under LMS:
- **Read/Study** — books, courses, external resources
- **Do** — take on stretch assignments that force use of weak skills in real work
- **Shadow** — observe high performers directly in context (e.g., sitting in on a strong PM's team meeting)
