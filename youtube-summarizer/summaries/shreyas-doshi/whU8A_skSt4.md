# How to Do Reverse Interviews

Video ID: `whU8A_skSt4`

## Summary
This video offers practical advice for job candidates — particularly senior individual contributors and executives — on how to assess the quality of talent and culture at a company during the interview process. The presenter argues that standard research (LinkedIn, Glassdoor) is insufficient, and that candidates should actively probe for signals of competence through the interview process itself, reverse interviews, and direct product evaluation. The core framework is that genuine talent always manifests in execution: product insight, pace, and quality. The advice is most relevant to senior product managers, CPOs, and experienced tech professionals with enough leverage to negotiate extended interview access.

## Key insights
- **Follow-up question quality is the best signal from interviewers.** Any company can pull smart-sounding questions from an interview bank, but follow-up questions must be generated in real time — they reveal whether the interviewer actually understood and engaged with your answer.
- **Process rigor is a two-way signal.** An overly easy hiring process isn't a compliment — it means the same low bar is applied to everyone, which degrades team quality. An excessively onerous process is also a red flag. Look for a balanced, appropriately rigorous process.
- **LinkedIn and Glassdoor research is table stakes**, not a differentiated approach — the presenter mentions it briefly as a baseline, not a key insight.
- **Conduct reverse interviews from a posture of curiosity, not arrogance.** The presenter spoke with 7–8 people at Twitter before accepting a role there. The goal is to understand what it's actually like to work there, what problems the team faces, and to better assess culture — not to signal your own importance.
- **Ask the hiring manager to name the 3–4 best people at the company and talk to them.** This tip came from a startup CEO during a CPO offer process. It gives you two benefits: a concrete benchmark of what "great" looks like internally, and pre-built relationships with top performers if you join.
- **Talk to the company's customers if you can** — product competence cannot be faked, and customers reveal the ground truth.
- **The ultimate signal is product execution.** Look at the company's products and ask: Is there a differentiated insight? Are they winning in the market? What is their pace and quality of output? This is the clearest and most honest indicator of talent quality — a company can claim greatness, but it must show up in what they ship.
- **Reverse interviews are a privilege of seniority.** The presenter explicitly notes this approach is not always available for entry-level roles — it applies at sufficiently senior levels where candidates have real leverage.

## Use cases
- A senior PM or CPO candidate evaluating multiple competing offers and needing a rigorous way to differentiate company cultures.
- Any experienced candidate who has received an offer but still feels uncertain about the team quality before accepting.
- A candidate preparing for final-round interviews who wants to ask better, more signal-generating questions.
- Someone joining a startup at a leadership level who wants to quickly identify internal allies and top performers before day one.
- A product professional assessing whether a company's claimed innovation culture is genuine or performative.

## Patterns & frameworks

**The Execution Signal Framework**
The presenter's core mental model: talent is real only if it shows up in three dimensions of execution — *insight* (is the product differentiated?), *pace* (how fast do they ship?), and *quality* (how well do they ship?). These are the same lenses a product manager applies to their own work, making them natural tools for candidate evaluation.

**Follow-Up Question Calibration**
A repeatable pattern for evaluating interviewers in real time: ignore the scripted question, pay attention to what they ask *after* your answer. Improvised follow-ups require genuine comprehension and intellectual engagement — they can't be borrowed from a question bank.

**The Top 3–4 Performer Request**
A specific, actionable tactic: once an offer is on the table, ask the hiring manager (or CEO) to name the 3–4 best people at the company across all functions, then request conversations with each. Works as both a culture/talent assessment tool and a relationship-building mechanism for day one.

**Curiosity-Framed Reverse Interviewing**
A mindset pattern for conducting reverse interviews: approach from genuine curiosity about day-to-day realities, challenges, and culture — not from a posture of demanding the company justify itself to you. This framing gets more honest answers and leaves a better impression.
