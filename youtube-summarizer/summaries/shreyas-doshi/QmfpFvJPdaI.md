# How Senior PM Leadership Interviews Actually Work: Part 1

Video ID: `QmfpFvJPdaI`

## Summary
This video is Part 1 of a series by Shreyas Doshi on how senior PM leadership interviews work, focusing on tactical advice for candidates pursuing head-of-product or senior PM roles. The core argument is that most candidates waste their interview time asking generic, self-centered questions, while standout candidates use their questions to add value to the interviewer and signal company-first thinking. Shreyas introduces two frameworks — the three PM leadership archetypes and Kent Beck's 3X product stages — to help candidates have more sophisticated conversations with founders and executives. The advice is most relevant to experienced PMs targeting senior roles at startups and mid-size companies, though parts apply to VP-level interviews at large tech companies as well.

---

## Key insights
- **Practice before your top targets**: Almost everyone is rusty in their first two or three interviews. Avoid interviewing with your top five target companies until you've had practice — this applies to roughly 70% of candidates, not all.
- **At startups, help founders clarify what they need**: Most candidates try to prove they're the best fit. The differentiating move is using your questions and perspective to help the founder or exec understand what they should even be looking for — this positions you as a peer, not just an applicant.
- **Know the three PM leader archetypes**: Operator, Craftsperson, and Visionary. Each fits different company contexts. Demonstrating fluency in this framework in conversation with a founder is a strong differentiator.
- **Kent Beck's 3X stages determine which archetype is needed**: Explore stage companies need a Visionary or Craftsperson; Expand stage needs a Craftsperson (transitioning to Operator in late Expand); Extract stage needs an Operator. Bringing in a Visionary again during Extract can drive reinvention.
- **Shreyas's personal Stripe example**: He joined Stripe when it was in an Expand/Craftsperson phase, which suited him. By the time the head of product role opened, Stripe had moved to a late Expand/Extract phase needing an Operator — and he recognized he wasn't the right fit, so he supported hiring someone else (who was a CFO background and became his boss).
- **Common end-of-interview questions are nearly worthless**: Questions like "What's the vision?", "What's the culture?", "Walk me through a recent product launch", or "What does your ideal candidate look like?" are generic, forgettable, and sometimes signal self-centeredness.
- **Most interviewers only leave 5 minutes for candidate questions** — you cannot change that constraint, but you can change how you use those 5 minutes or convert them into a follow-up conversation.
- **Three golden questions** replace the generic ones and make the interviewer actually think:
  1. *"What aspects of the product make sense for you to continue owning after you hire the head of product?"* — surfaces potential conflict zones and signals you're thinking about what's right for the company, not about territorial ownership.
  2. *"What is the one superpower you want this person to have?"* — forces the interviewer to prioritize, giving you genuinely useful signal rather than a rehearsed job description recitation.
  3. *"What are the one or two things that truly matter, that this person needs to score an 11 out of 10 on?"* — far more powerful than "What does success look like?" because it forces prioritization and avoids sounding corporate or self-focused.
- **The golden questions work best** when talking to a founder, senior exec, or VP — not when talking to a GPM at a large tech company who lacks strategic visibility into the role.

## Use cases
- Senior PMs preparing for head-of-product interviews at startups or growth-stage companies
- PMs interviewing with founders who haven't fully articulated what they need in a product leader
- Candidates at any level who want to differentiate themselves in the "do you have questions for me?" portion of an interview
- PMs evaluating whether a company's current product stage matches their own leadership archetype before applying
- Hiring managers or founders who want a framework for thinking about what kind of PM leader they actually need
- VPs at large tech companies hiring senior product leaders onto their teams

## Patterns & frameworks

**Three PM Leadership Archetypes (Shreyas Doshi)**
- *Operator*: Executes at scale, runs process and systems, suited for mature/extract-stage products
- *Craftsperson*: Translates vision into concrete, differentiated product features; suited for expand-stage products
- *Visionary*: Sets product direction and inspires; suited for early explore-stage or turnaround scenarios
- Use case: Match the archetype to the company's current product stage; helps both candidates self-assess fit and helps founders articulate what they need

**Kent Beck's 3X Framework**
- Three sequential product stages: *Explore* (finding what works), *Expand* (scaling what works), *Extract* (maximizing value from what's established)
- Each stage requires fundamentally different leadership, prioritization logic, and optimization targets — copying playbooks across stages is a mistake
- The framework originated when Beck was at Facebook; he is also the creator of Extreme Programming (XP)

**The Three Golden Interview Questions**
- A repeatable pattern for the end-of-interview "your questions" slot, specifically when speaking with a founder or senior executive
- Each question is designed to (a) make the interviewer think rather than recite, (b) signal company-first orientation, and (c) yield genuinely useful signal for the candidate
- Questions: superpower needed → what to continue owning → what must score 11/10
