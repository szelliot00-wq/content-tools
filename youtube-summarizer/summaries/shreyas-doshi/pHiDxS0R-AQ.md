# The "Eyes Light Up" Test: How Shreyas Decides What to Teach

Video ID: `pHiDxS0R-AQ`

## Summary
Shreyas Doshi explains his personal process for deciding what content to teach in his courses and advising work. The core method is introspective rather than analytical: he identifies ideas from his experience that feel genuinely novel — things never shared in public or private forums — and then validates them by recalling whether people's "eyes lit up" when he shared those ideas in real conversations. The interviewer adds a structural breakdown, noting that Shreyas combines deep personal expertise with an observed demand signal from watching how people respond to advice in the moment. This framework is most relevant to educators, coaches, consultants, and practitioners who are deciding what knowledge to productize or share publicly.

## Key insights
- Shreyas uses an **introspective, internally-driven** approach to content selection, not an externally-driven or data-analytics-driven one.
- The primary filter is novelty: he asks whether an idea has been shared before in **any** forum — public or private — and prioritizes ideas that feel genuinely unique.
- The "eyes light up" test is his demand-validation mechanism: if people visibly lit up during 1-on-1 advising or coaching conversations, that's evidence the idea is useful, practical, and applicable.
- The test implicitly checks for **differentiation** — the key question is whether someone would get this perspective from anywhere else. If the answer is no, it's worth teaching.
- He applies this across two domains where he has deep expertise: **product sense/product strategy** (advising founders) and **career coaching** (coaching PM leaders).
- The interviewer identifies two structural components in Shreyas's process: (1) **expertise selection** — picking a topic where you have genuine, differentiated skill — and (2) **observed demand** — watching where people naturally gravitate and what they respond to in real conversations.

## Use cases
- A practitioner deciding what to include in a course, newsletter, or talk they're building.
- A coach or advisor trying to identify which parts of their tacit knowledge are most worth making explicit.
- A consultant scoping a service offering and needing to validate whether their angle is differentiated enough to matter.
- A PM or PM leader evaluating whether their hard-won experience in strategy or career growth is "teachable" and in demand.
- Anyone moving from practitioner to educator and wondering where to start.

## Patterns & frameworks
**The "Eyes Light Up" Test**
A qualitative demand-validation heuristic. Before teaching something, recall past 1-on-1 conversations where you shared that idea — did the other person visibly respond with excitement or recognition? If yes, that's evidence the idea is both useful and differentiated. It functions as a retrospective signal, not a prospective survey.

**Novelty Filter (Internal Screening)**
Before even applying the eyes-light-up test, Shreyas screens ideas for whether they've been shared anywhere before (publicly or privately). This ensures the content is genuinely additive — not a repackaging of existing frameworks — which is the prerequisite for differentiation.

**Expertise + Observed Demand (Interviewer's Decomposition)**
The interviewer reframes Shreyas's process as a two-part structure: first, anchor in an area of genuine, above-average expertise; second, let real audience reactions — not surveys or keyword research — reveal where demand actually lives. Together these produce content that is both credible and wanted.
