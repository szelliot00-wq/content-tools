# The 1% Rule

Video ID: `irDSNU7e6Cc`

## Summary
This video presents a career tactic called "The 1% Rule" — the idea that professionals should always dedicate at least 1% of their energy to staying open to new job opportunities, even when not actively looking. The speaker (Shreyas Doshi, a PM leader) argues that the psychological difference between 0% and 1% openness is enormous, and that staying at 0% causes people to miss transformational career moves. The video is part of a broader series on inbound job searching and is most relevant to mid-to-senior level product managers and tech professionals navigating career growth.

## Key insights
- There is a significant psychological gap between being 0% open and 1% open to new roles — 0% means you mentally shut down entirely, while 1% keeps you receptive to inbound opportunities.
- The 1% rule is specifically about **inbound** job searching — you're not actively hunting, you're staying open to being found.
- A scripted response to executive recruiters lets you manage inbound without feeling overwhelmed: "I'm not looking right now because [reason], but if you come across a once-in-a-lifetime PM leadership opportunity, reach out anytime."
- That script reliably prompts a follow-up question from the recruiter: "What does a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity look like for you?" — which is exactly the conversation you want to have.
- Your answer to that question should be anchored to your **lighthouse goal** (a 3-year career north star), even if you don't share the goal directly. Example given: "The first PM role at Facebook in 2007, or Uber in 2011."
- The more senior you are, the **longer** you should be willing to stay passive in the market — resist the urge to close a role as fast as possible, because the right senior opportunity takes time to appear.
- Job search is not binary (looking vs. not looking). There is a spectrum: *not interested → not looking but open to connections → open to the right opportunity → very open to exploring → actively interviewing*. The speaker aims to stay in the middle range at all times.
- All difficult career conversations are ultimately **messaging problems** — finding the right words for a tricky situation makes the problem disappear.

## Use cases
- Senior PMs or tech leaders who are happily employed but want to avoid being blindsided by missed opportunities.
- Professionals preparing to engage with executive recruiters or VC talent partners without committing to an active search.
- Anyone who feels overwhelmed by recruiter outreach and wants a clean, repeatable script to manage it gracefully.
- People who are unclear on what their ideal next role looks like and need a framework to articulate it.
- Mid-career professionals who have historically treated job searching as binary and want a more nuanced, always-on approach.
- Professionals preparing for a senior job search who need to recalibrate their timeline expectations.

## Patterns & frameworks

**The 1% Rule**
Always reserve at least 1% of your mental energy for career awareness, even when fully employed. The goal is to never fully close yourself off from inbound opportunity. This is a passive posture, not an active search.

**The Recruiter Script**
A repeatable message for executive recruiters: acknowledge you're not looking, give a concrete reason, but leave a specific door open ("once-in-a-lifetime opportunity"). This prevents recruiter spam while inviting the conversations worth having. It reliably triggers the recruiter to ask you to define your ideal role — a productive conversation on your terms.

**The Lighthouse Goal as Career Compass**
A 3-year directional goal ("lighthouse") that you use privately to evaluate opportunities and articulate what a transformational role would look like. You don't share the goal itself, but you use it to answer the recruiter's follow-up question with specificity.

**The Job Search Spectrum**
A five-stage model replacing the binary "looking / not looking" framing:
1. Not interested at all
2. Not looking, but open to great connections
3. Open to the right opportunity
4. Very open to exploring
5. Actively interviewing

The speaker recommends staying at stages 2–3 permanently, using the 1% rule to avoid ever falling to stage 1.

**Senior-Role Time Horizon Heuristic**
The more senior the role, the longer you should be willing to stay passive in the market. Resist the instinct to close a role quickly — patience yields better-fit opportunities at senior levels.
