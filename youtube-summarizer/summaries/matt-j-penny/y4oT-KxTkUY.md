# STOP Your AI Motion Graphics Looking Like SLOP in 4 Steps

Video ID: `y4oT-KxTkUY`

## Summary
This video outlines a four-step framework for improving AI-generated motion graphics so they don't look like generic "slop." The creator, Matt, draws on nine months of experience producing thousands of AI motion graphics to identify the tools, processes, and habits that separate professional-looking output from instantly-dismissable AI content. The framework centers on selecting the right GitHub repos/skills, finding targeted inspiration, consolidating preferences into a "video manifest," and running iterative feedback loops. It is most relevant to content creators, video editors, and marketers using Claude or similar AI tools to produce motion graphics and video content at scale.

## Key insights
- **Generic AI output looks like "slop" by default** — the difference between slop and professional-looking graphics comes down to giving AI the right tools and the right processes, not just better prompts.
- **GitHub repos as passive quality boosters**: Specific repos installed into Claude improve output automatically. Examples cited:
  - *unslop-motion-graphics* — strips the generic AI aesthetic
  - *impeccable skill* — adds an extra layer of design sophistication
  - *taste skill* — attempts to give Claude aesthetic judgment
- **Style-specific skills unlock distinct visual modes**: Unlike passive repos, these are used selectively depending on the desired look:
  - *Three.js framework* — enables 3D motion graphics (less associated with AI slop)
  - *React Three Rapier* — adds realistic physics simulation, useful for explainer videos
  - *Anatomy skill* — builds and disassembles 3D anatomical models
  - *Isometric look (Lemo Opus)* — produces isometric-style visuals
  - *Blender MCP* — full 3D scene and world creation for CGI-level output
- **"Slop" is subjective** — it means low-effort and recognizably AI-generated to the viewer; the fix is making output match aesthetics people actually like.
- **Inspiration sourcing is a core process step**, not an afterthought. Recommended sources:
  - *Motion Design* — hosts motion graphic examples with prompts attached
  - *Scenario Motion Loops* — library of reusable effects
  - *Motion in Design* — similar resource
  - YouTube/Instagram/TikTok URLs can be fed directly into Claude for style analysis
- **Do not copy styles wholesale** — the goal is to mix and match elements from multiple sources and overlay your own brand colors and fonts to produce something unique.
- **The Video Manifest is the central organizing tool** — an HTML document (more powerful than a standard markdown style guide) that stores everything: preferred skills, tools, processes, inspiration videos, templates, voice preferences, music preferences, transition styles, and more. It lets Claude recreate your editing style from scratch on any new project.
- **The Video Manifest enables near-autonomous editing** — once built, Matt claims he can hand Claude a raw recording and get ~90% of editing done in ~10 minutes.
- **Iteration is the most overlooked step** — Matt uses a tool called *Polmir Pro* (described as similar to DaVinci Resolve, CapCut, or Premiere Pro) which supports in-timeline comments that AI can read and act on.
- **A post-production iteration skill reviews all comments left during editing**, identifies recurring mistakes and preferences, and writes those learnings back into the Video Manifest automatically — so each future project starts with fewer errors.
- **This compounding feedback loop** is how Matt reduces video editing time to roughly one hour per video with minimal personal input.

## Patterns & frameworks

**1. The Four-Step Anti-Slop Framework**
A repeatable process for improving AI motion graphics:
1. Install the right repos/skills (passive quality boosters + selective style tools)
2. Find targeted inspiration from curated sites or real video URLs
3. Build and maintain a Video Manifest that consolidates all preferences
4. Run iterative feedback loops after every project to compound improvement over time

**2. Passive vs. Selective Tools**
A mental model for categorizing AI enhancement tools: *passive* tools (like unslop, impeccable, taste) improve output automatically once installed; *selective* tools (like Three.js, React Three Rapier, Blender MCP) are invoked only when their specific visual style is desired.

**3. The Video Manifest**
An HTML-based master preference document that functions like an extended style guide. It stores not just visual brand elements (colors, fonts) but behavioral preferences (voice style, music choices, transition types, animation pacing) and links to inspiration. Acts as persistent context so Claude produces consistent, personalized output without re-briefing each session.

**4. Comment-Driven Iteration Loop**
Using Polmir Pro's in-timeline comment feature, the creator annotates mistakes and preferences during editing review. A dedicated iteration skill then reads those comments post-production, extracts the learnings, and writes them back into the Video Manifest — creating a self-improving system where each project raises the baseline quality floor for the next one.
