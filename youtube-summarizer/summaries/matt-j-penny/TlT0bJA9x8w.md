# This New AI Video Editor Will Save You HOURS

Video ID: `TlT0bJA9x8w`

## Summary
This video demonstrates InVideo, an AI-powered browser-based video editor that consolidates the full editing workflow into a single tool. The creator walks through editing a raw talking-head video from start to finished export, using an AI agent named "Gary" to handle silence removal, bad take cutting, subtitle generation, B-roll ideation, AI image creation (via GPT Image 2.5), and video animation (via Seedance 2.0). The core argument is that AI has eliminated the most time-consuming parts of video editing, reducing hours of work to a few prompts. It is most relevant to solo content creators, YouTubers, and small teams who edit talking-head or educational video content regularly.

## Key insights
- InVideo runs entirely in the browser with no setup, plugin installation, or third-party tool connections required — it handles up to 200 GB of video per project.
- An AI agent (user-named, e.g. "Gary") is configured in just two prompts and then executes multi-step editing tasks autonomously.
- Silence and bad take removal — typically 30–60 minutes of manual work on a long video — was completed automatically in under a minute.
- Subtitle/caption generation with per-word animation was added via a single chat prompt, a task that historically required outsourcing or hours of manual work.
- B-roll ideation is identified as a **weakness**: the AI produces generic ideas and the creator explicitly recommends either doing ideation yourself or reviewing AI suggestions before approving.
- Images are generated using GPT Image 2.5 directly inside the editor, inserted at AI-identified timestamp positions in the timeline automatically.
- Static images can be animated into video clips using Seedance 2.0, also from within the same tool — no external video generation service needed.
- The editor UI mirrors familiar tools (Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, CapCut), so manual overrides (color correction, manual cuts, font changes) are accessible alongside AI actions.
- Export options include MP4 or an editable project file compatible with Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, and DaVinci Resolve — useful for hybrid workflows where rough cuts are done in InVideo and finishing is done elsewhere.
- A 50% discount on certain plans was available at time of recording (link in description).
- The creator's honest take: the tool is technically strong but still requires human creative involvement, particularly for prompts and B-roll decisions, to get high-quality outputs.

## Patterns & frameworks

**AI-Assisted Rough Cut Workflow**
A repeatable process: (1) upload raw footage, (2) prompt the AI agent to remove silences and bad takes, (3) review the auto-cut timeline, (4) make manual adjustments as needed. Reduces the most tedious phase of editing to a single prompt.

**Prompt → Review → Approve Loop**
For higher-judgment tasks like B-roll selection, the creator uses a two-step pattern: first ask the AI for *ideas or options*, review them yourself, then instruct the AI to execute on the approved selections. This keeps human creative judgment in the loop while offloading execution.

**Human Creativity + AI Execution Split**
An implicit framework throughout: use AI for technical/repetitive tasks (cutting, transcription, caption rendering, file export) and retain human involvement for creative decisions (prompt writing, B-roll concept selection, style choices). The creator explicitly flags B-roll ideation as a place where this split breaks down if left fully to AI.

**All-in-One Tool vs. Connected Stack**
The video positions InVideo against a multi-tool workflow (e.g., separate transcription tool + Pictory or TopView for images + manual timeline work) and argues that consolidation into one platform reduces friction and setup cost, even if individual specialist tools might outperform it on specific tasks.
